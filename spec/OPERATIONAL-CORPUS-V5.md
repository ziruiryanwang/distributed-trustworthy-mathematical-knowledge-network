## Product and scope remain unchanged

The commons supports independent investigators acquiring, checking, using and contributing formal mathematics. Lean 4 is the initial basis. There is no necessary central server, universal theorem ledger, common research goal, intention assessment or unsolved-mathematics task registry. Local heuristics may improve useful work; they cannot supply missing mathematical evidence.

This amendment targets two places where a formally bounded design can still fail ordinary work: native reconstruction after changing proof representatives, and scaling a finite index scope into actual sustained service. It also makes the foundation/bootstrap and complete-export paths more explicit. Safe refusal remains necessary, but is not mistaken for successful mathematical reuse.

## Acyclicity and proof irrelevance do not prove unchanged replay

PI1's source-earliest construction proves a structural fact: under its finite complete supported source-graph premise, one can choose an acyclic materialization plan. It does not prove that every original fixed proof term checks after semantic proof occurrences are replaced by those representatives.

Use three distinct relations:

- A source expression has a complete well-scoped canonical candidate translation.
- The abstract proof-irrelevant theory relates two proof-valued terms at the same proposition and supports suitable explicit mathematical transports.
- The actual qualified kernel conversion procedure succeeds on a particular pair of terms under a particular realized environment.

The third is an actual computation with native rules and costs. It is not inferred from the first two. In particular, changing an accessibility proof used in data computation can change whether an otherwise fixed reflexivity proof passes that conversion procedure. Proofs can be logically irrelevant while their opacity or representation matters to computation.

Accordingly, the v4 proof-replacement discussion is an abstract typing/correspondence motivation, not an operational completeness theorem for the pinned checker or unchanged exported bodies. Final actual checking, exact support and source-correspondence outcomes remain mandatory. No native rejection is overridden by claiming that proof irrelevance should have made the terms equal.

The H computation note supplies a source-derived paper family using a safe opaque accessibility proof and a transparent alternative. It selects finite computation-aware representative attempts and actual explicit equality/transport repairs. These are new checked replay/proof artifacts where necessary, not inherited acceptance. The countercase is not an observed compiled Lean project or an executed exploit.

Concretely, if a canonical alias selects an opaque-backed proof p of an accessibility proposition while another retained instance q exposes a constructor, the fixed data conversion `Z(p)=0` can get stuck even though `Z(q)` reduces to zero. An actual checked proof of `p=q` followed by congruence for Z supplies an explicit intermediate that the converter need not discover. This can repair the result at its original statement and DefinitionID, with a new proof instance and its complete additional support. It does not make an opaque value transparent. A finite family can require incompatible source preferences, so selecting the original witness at every occurrence is not a universal one-B solution.

REDUCE1 keeps source-occurrence and failed-conversion records locally, grounds every candidate realization again, and rechecks affected types, values and generated groups in a coherent environment. Its example search envelope is 16 complete plans, four alternatives per demanded judgment and 256 diagnostic/repair attempts, all inside actual aggregate native/work/memory budgets. These are adjustable engineering caps, not a promise of broad-library success. Checked equation lemmas or locally constructed explicit transports are allowed; foreign tactics, fabricated assumptions and stale conversion caches are not.

## Complete producer export and native group recovery

EXTRACT1 operates on an immutable complete source cut with actual theorem/opaque bodies and stable reference resolution. It maintains separate semantic-name and proof-instance maps. Universe order, exact member selectors and the judgment telescope split remain explicit; arbitrary normalization is not treated as identity-preserving deduplication.

A producer may recover an inductive group from a captured completed native declaration input or form a candidate from completed group/constructor records. Actual ordered group membership and constructor indexes govern the mapping, not encounter order or recursor-like source names. The receiver regenerates the group through the checked native operation and reconciles its full inventory/support. A failed exact source correspondence is not renamed a successful original-interface import merely because some different target mathematics checks.

Source correspondence is scoped, not one universal round-trip bit. The export and computation notes distinguish semantic descriptor correspondence, actual target validity, original-body preservation, selected conversion preservation, generated-interface correspondence, frontend usability and current reusable evidence. Derived inductive flags (`numNested`, `isRec`, `isReflexive`) participate in the stated exact inventory comparison; publisher-supplied flags never become native authority.

Candidate caches bind the exact source unit, normalization/recovery interpretation, target foundation and direct resolved reference boundary. A source spelling or unchanged source file is not a sufficient cache key. Conversely, an unrelated global workspace update need not invalidate an unchanged exact unit. These are candidate transformation caches, never native acceptance or current-use authority.

The pinned Lean source is still `293d5d0c0c3f3dded4688b3ccd6a33939ac5102b`. NativeRules code 4 still selects PI1 with HEIGHT1. The source audit expands the mandatory qualification inventory rather than assigning a new code to unchanged constructors/rules.

Every exact FoundationID binds its actual SeedProfile, NativeRules and ReservedNames records. A changed seed/export/name inventory changes those commitments and therefore its FoundationID; no already frozen qualified foundation retains its ID through an inventory change. No actual final bootstrap/image digest has been derived or tested in this concept phase.

Kernel-reachable exact-name behavior is distinguished from frontend-only convenience. In particular, functions reached by the kernel's annotation-consuming or eager-reduction path cannot be dismissed as inert elaborator metadata simply because their logical bodies are identity functions. Unsafe native evaluation remains prohibited; identifying additional names is not authorization to enable them.

The seed uses its explicitly name-local, dependency-ordered descriptor grammar. It does not contain ordinary F-bound PI1 objects whose identifiers recursively depend on the FoundationID that already commits to the seed. A mandatory finite seed closure can expose its exact checked logical declarations without exposing executable tactics/plugins or treating all installed axioms as used by every root. Per-export actual support remains derived.

The exact added seed roots are `outParam`, `semiOutParam`, `optParam`, `autoParam` and `eagerReduce`, with full safe logical closure. `autoParam` includes its actual `Lean.Syntax` datatype support; syntax values are not executed. All retained checked logical seed declarations and generated members are exported. The closed-name barrier excludes `Lean.reduceBool` and `Lean.reduceNat`, and the exact axiom whitelist separately excludes other compiler-oracle axioms regardless of spelling. Ordinary source-private helpers are not blanket-prohibited.

Bootstrap staging requires the checked Nat group before natural literals, the complete conservative String/Char/List expansion basis before string literals, Bool before predicates that can produce its constructors, and Eq before the one quotient initialization. ## Shared feature coverage without shared row ownership

The v4 FULL_FEATURE predicate concerns one explicit finite target scope and its emitted token union. Arbitrarily many such scopes at one provider can overlap in tokens. The routing protocol still permits only one provider/key row at a cell and one exact owning group; different scopes cannot each claim ownership of that same row.

Select the H local shared-coverage contract. It keeps the original scope and group ownership rules and separately tracks which selected target scopes are covered by actual eligible provider/token rows owned elsewhere in the same authorized provider's indexed scope family. A borrowed row is not inserted twice and its original group renewal still requires an eligible exact anchor in its own scope. The aggregate coverage assertion is a new local derived predicate, not a relabeling of a PARTIAL individual scope as FULL_FEATURE.

Scope consolidation and ownership transitions consume actual membership, group, marker, staging and renewal resources. Old anchors cannot be discarded while their ownership/coverage obligations still rely on them. Existing one-owner row rules can require a replacement path or a bounded reported coverage gap during handoff; no atomic cross-group move is fabricated out of unrelated calls.

During a sequential handoff, old and new groups can own different holder copies of the same token. The local ledger records the intended next owner separately from each row's actual owner, and reports TRANSFERRING/PARTIAL rather than the settled single-owner coverage predicate. Those mixed copies can still serve useful discovery; the status does not assert a physical global outage. The new settled owner is recorded only when its required exact row observations hold. Withdrawn/expired old groups remain charged until actual reclamation.

This local aggregation/consolidation mechanism introduces no new wire opcode or native truth authority. Larger scopes require actual supplied capacities and explicit local scope commitments. A million targets do not fit a 128-target allowance merely because the software labels the collection one library.

## What resource adequacy must count

Storage/custody nodes, native-eligible index providers and untrusted routing holders have distinct roles. The current provider-public index contract requires its actual checked retained evidence; a metadata-only broker cannot pretend to satisfy it. Within one node, a shared native dependency can be stored once under correct ownership accounting. Across independent nodes, replication and cold checking are real work.

Metadata cost includes target-token postings, distinct provider/token ads, selected and transient replica rows, holder-group state, renewals, ancestor markers, snapshots and failed attempts. Native cost additionally includes actual complete protected closures, cold checks and changed-realization reconstruction. Average occupancy is not a maximum-load guarantee, and successful network calls are not successful mathematical applications.

The H workload is a fully stated arithmetic/capacity example, not measured performance. Its purpose is to reveal necessary supplied resources and failure regimes. Practical usefulness and sustainable resource provision remain subject to actual qualification after implementation is authorized.

For its declared 1,048,576 targets and two qualified copies across 1,024 slots, with 16 tokens per target (eight common, eight target-specific), the initial stocks are 33,554,432 postings, 16,785,408 provider/token ads and 67,141,632 chosen four-copy routing rows. The fixture additionally assumes a 2-GiB shared core per qualified slot and at most 256 KiB incremental evidence per target, giving up to 2.5 TiB retained native evidence before working sets and metadata. These are explicitly supplied fixtures, not properties inferred for mathlib.

With 100,000 initially cold clients each needing that core, onboarding alone moves about 195.31 TiB. If all 1,024 slots supply 8 GiB/day, dedicating their entire traffic grant to those cores still takes at least about 24.4 days. Native checking can be more restrictive: 2,048 targets consume the slot's entire 28,800-CPU-second day at a mean 14.0625 seconds per target even if everything else is free. Actual core qualification, maintenance and query work lower that threshold. A warm-only query demonstration must not conceal these acquisition costs.

The current choice of full native retention/checking at every qualified mathematical index provider is deliberately visible. An untrusted metadata-only candidate broker could reduce duplicated indexing costs without deciding mathematical truth, but needs a distinct acquisition/discovery/advertisement contract. It is not selected by silently weakening G's PubliclyServed/native-eligibility predicate. That architecture question remains open to a separately audited concept revision.
