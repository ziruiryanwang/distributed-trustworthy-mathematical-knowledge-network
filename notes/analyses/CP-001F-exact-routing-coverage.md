# CP-001F: explicit exact-query routing coverage

Date: 2026-09-20  
Status: selected fine-index amendment for independent paper audit; no implementation or throughput claim

## Why coarse routing is insufficient

The [ROUTE1 catalog layer](CP-001F-dissemination-and-routing.md) repairs neighborhood isolation but its 16 coarse provider keys cannot cover an arbitrarily rich mathematical collection. Suppose P honest providers advertise the same foundation and common structural keys, while precisely one has a sought exact JudgmentID J. If J has no fine routing membership, all routing observations are compatible with any provider holding it. After q distinct provider-index probes an adversarial placement can remain undiscovered when q<P; with a uniformly located target, success is at most q/P. Faster DHT lookup does not repair this information deficit.

The selected repair is a separate **FULL_EXACT finite scope**: actual routing memberships for every exact JudgmentID in that explicitly chosen scope, with measured preparation, placement and refresh coverage. The initial scope has at most 4096 judgments; a larger profile can admit up to 65536 when its complete record, placement, maintenance and native costs are supplied. These numbers are example local envelopes, not enough for every library or a universal bound on mathematics.

This is not a mandate to index all mathematics at each node. A large provider must shard its selected collection across willing index supply or admit a larger measured profile; it cannot call an unadvertised million-judgment collection fully exact-routed merely because its foundation fallback exists. Exact-key publication removes the forced P-provider scan for a correctly placed exact target under the route assumptions. It does not solve relevance search when the useful statement/JudgmentID is not known.

## Meaning of FULL_EXACT and local selection

`FineScope = [1,FoundationID,orderedJudgmentIDs]` is an immutable finite owner-local scope. IDs are sorted, duplicate-free, all in the exact foundation, and drawn from actual public indexed judgments with currently retained permitted native evidence. Its scopeID is the SHA-256 digest of ASCII `DTMKN-FINE-SCOPE` followed by its canonical payload. This is a local scope commitment, not a new native object, a remote CAS read capability or a mathematical theorem.

One selected provider/key membership uses the existing signed ProviderAd with `K=[1,FoundationID,JudgmentID]`. Its anchor descriptor/replay actually names that exact judgment. Multiple local proofs of J do not require separate provider rows: ordinary exact FIND returns the provider's available proof alternatives. At the producer, a changed anchor uses a higher ad revision and rechecks current disclosure/retention before preparing replacement.

The scope mode FULL_EXACT describes **which targets require membership**, not an unconditional assertion that every target is currently visible everywhere. Its bounded local status separates:

- target count in the immutable scope;
- targets with currently prepared exact local ads;
- targets with complete recorded placement paths and the age of their observations;
- targets pending initial placement, renewal, replacement, or withdrawal;
- partial/failed paths and resource/route/admission failures;
- currently public/native-usable targets versus historical/degraded targets.

`ALL_PLACEMENTS_OBSERVED` means every target had the required paid placement observations at the reported local observation boundary, under explicitly reported ages. It is not a certificate that remote machines still store the rows at delivery time. No responder's monotonic ticks are turned into synchronized global expiry. A placement is useful operational evidence, not native acceptance or a guarantee of future service.

Default nomination chooses a finite local public collection cut and includes **every exact judgment in that cut**, not just its most frequent symbols. If that complete cut does not fit, the owner selects a smaller explicit cut or funds a larger one; the software reports omitted scope instead of silently truncating an advertised FULL_EXACT collection. New public judgments enter a bounded pending-next-scope queue derived from the existing public index; queue saturation sets an explicit uncovered-new-content boundary/count rather than allocating one unlimited protected job per theorem. There are at most four active fine scopes and 4096 aggregate exact targets in the initial profile. Replacement scopes and their temporary double-index costs require fresh local admission.

## Membership placement and holder groups

Fine keys use ROUTE1's exact cell hashing, 0–32-byte provider-prefix sharding, iterative nearest-holder lookup and parent-marker paths. The selected fine target is **four admitted copies** among the route's up to 16 responsive holders, not a claim that four keys are independent organizations. Process those holders in exact distance/NodeID order and stop placing new copies once four succeed; failures can move to later holders. Fewer than four or missing parent markers gives PARTIAL. The receiver-side discovery contract remains querying the relevant selected holder set under the base route assumptions.

Provider ads with different exact keys can land at the same holder. A `ProviderHolderGroup` lets that holder retain those distinct memberships under one existing local lease, rather than requiring a separate recurring renewal RPC for every row. It does not make different target keys share one DHT position or erase their initial routing/insertion work.

`GroupState = [holderNodeID,groupID,providerNodeID,scopeID,FoundationID,stateVersion,lifecycle,memberRoot,memberCount,memberBytes,leaseDeadline,allocationHandle]`.

The holder creates groupID as exactly 24 opaque bytes: its durable 16-byte authority-generation identifier followed by a never-reused uint64 big-endian group counter. It checks counter exhaustion before allocation; normal restart preserves this history, while uncertain rollback uses the existing new-authority/re-admission boundary, not replay of old IDs. State versions are checked uint64, start at zero for the empty OPEN state, and never wrap. Group lookup is by exact holder/groupID and authenticated provider; one active group exists per `(providerNodeID,scopeID)` at a holder. ScopeID is exactly 32 bytes and is an immutable namespace binding; the holder need not obtain or trust the source's entire native scope. It validates each PUT key as an exact-judgment key in the group's foundation. Actual membership in the owner's chosen FineScope is the producer's separately checked publication obligation.

`memberRoot` commits to the canonical finite map `(cell,providerNodeID) -> ProviderAdCID`. Select operational map node type `0x8023/1`, with keyDigest `SHA256(ASCII("DTMKN-ROUTE1-GROUP-KEY") || encode([cell,providerNodeID]))`. Empty is `[2]`; leaf is `[0,keyDigest32,cell,providerNodeID,ProviderAdCID]`; branch is `[1,splitBit,prefixBytes,leftCID,rightCID]`. Keys have 256 bits; branches split at the first differing bit, have two nonempty properly partitioned children, and use the minimum canonical prefix encoding with zero unused low bits, as in the other selected Patricia contracts. Nodes have at most 2048 canonical bytes; the exact-judgment-only leaf fits this bound. Child CIDs must use `0x8023/1`. The full original key is retained and checked; an unexpected key-hash collision fails that mutation rather than treating different membership keys as equal. The group root is operational bookkeeping, not a native witness map, public CAS read permission or truth certificate. Empty and changed roots have exact locally derived counts/bytes; a caller-supplied count is not authority.

A cell/provider row belongs either to one live group or to the base ungrouped lease, never two silently conflicting owners. A PUT encountering a live row owned by another group/ungrouped lease yields ROW_OWNER_CONFLICT; it does not steal that allocation. Producers choose nonoverlapping active scopes for the same exact key and explicitly migrate/withdraw obsolete rows. At the same holder/key, scope replacement must first complete an authorized DROP/old-group transition before inserting the new owner; this contract does not provide seamless make-before-break there. Other already placed holders can cover the interval only if those actual copies remain available. A scope transition cannot count a previously owned row as newly placed without a successful appropriate holder operation. The underlying native proof remains retained independently of route-index movement.

This amendment explicitly restricts base ROUTE_PUT_AD/code 12 and ROUTE_DROP_AD/code 15 to ungrouped rows: an existing group-owned row returns ROW_OWNER_CONFLICT even when the same provider sends the base call. They cannot bypass the group's map, count/byte aggregate or stateVersion. GROUP_BATCH can mutate only rows belonging to its exact group, or insert an eligible absent row under that group. Expired/CLOSED replacement uses the old-group/storage-generation fence below; it never mutates the new row through an old group's delayed cleanup or a base operation against stale ownership.

## Negotiated bounded group operations

Capability 6 means ROUTE1 fine groups and implies syntax support for capability 5. It is negotiated through reciprocal existing HELLO/HELLO_REPLY exchanges on the authenticated connection, exactly as in the base note. Client syntax support is not an obligation to host groups. ServiceCall remains `0x8004/2`; new codes 16–19 below are sent only after both capabilities are observed. No old code or outer wire version changes.

| Code | Operation | Arguments |
|---:|---|---|
| 16 | `ROUTE_GROUP_OPEN` | `[scopeID,FoundationID,requestedLeaseMillis]` |
| 17 | `ROUTE_GROUP_BATCH` | `[groupID,expectedStateVersion,expectedMemberRoot,orderedMutations]` |
| 18 | `ROUTE_GROUP_RENEW` | `[groupID,expectedStateVersion,expectedMemberRoot,requestedLeaseMillis]` |
| 19 | `ROUTE_GROUP_CLOSE` | `[groupID,expectedStateVersion,expectedMemberRoot]` |

These are provider-authenticated paid calls. Exact groupID/provider/holder/foundation checks happen before mutation. Requested lease milliseconds are positive and at most 172800000; checked version/count arithmetic does not wrap. A copied ad signature or historical group receipt cannot renew or mutate a group. Nonmembers do not receive private group inventory/status merely because they know a groupID. Group errors use the base note's selected `[reasonCode,groupObservationOption]` body and inherited top-level RESULT code mapping, not newly invented top-level codes for ROW_OWNER_CONFLICT/UNKNOWN_GROUP/EXPIRED or other prose reason names.

OPEN creates an empty counted group with a finite lease and no provider rows, or returns the already active matching provider/scope group's scoped state **without extending its lease**. Group creation does not reserve an arbitrarily large future index or authorize later work. PUT capacity is admitted separately for the actual new rows/bytes. A source cannot reset expiry or mint another duplicate group by repeating OPEN.

Mutations are `[0,cell,signedProviderAd]` for PUT or `[1,cell,expectedAdCID]` for DROP. A batch contains at most 64 mutations, at most 64 KiB for its entire control request body, and at most one mutation per exact membership key; rows sort by canonical membership key. Both limits apply, so 64 maximum-size ads do not fit and must be split. The group profile explicitly permits the existing 64-KiB hard control-body ceiling instead of the ordinary 16-KiB local default for this operation. No body stream or unbounded record array is introduced.

PUT verifies signature, actual provider identity, group foundation/key, cell-prefix membership, exact revision rule and row ownership. Existing equal-revision/equal-byte rows are idempotent within this new paid operation; equal revision/different bytes or stale revision fails the whole batch. DROP names the current exact expected ad; absent/already-removed rows have an explicit expected-state outcome rather than a silent new lease. No batch fetches native evidence or follows advertisements.

The holder stages the bounded decoded batch and reserves its actual delta storage, metadata, byte-time through the already existing group deadline, transaction and reply allocations. One authoritative transaction rechecks group version/root/lease, all expected rows, local disclosure and capacity, then either applies the **entire** batch or applies none. A changed state advances stateVersion once, updates exact memberRoot/count/bytes, and returns a result binding the exact mutation call. BATCH never extends the lease. Partial per-row application is not permitted; a caller that wants smaller independent progress submits new smaller paid batches.

The successful result for each group operation is

`[holderNodeID,groupID,scopeID,stateVersion,lifecycleCode,memberRoot,memberCount,memberBytes,leaseObservation]`,

where lifecycleCode is 0=ACTIVE, 1=CLOSED, or 2=EXPIRED. `leaseObservation` is `[0]` for no active lease or `[1,localObservationID,grantedRemainingMillis]` for the holder's exact commit-boundary observation. It is not a receiver-wall-clock lease. The immutable common RESULT metadata binds the exact call/result; reply delivery remains a separate paid one-shot allocation. A conflict returns a bounded provider-private current group observation for reconciliation, not an automatic retry or automatic mutation against the newer version.

RENEW requires the existing exact active group version and memberRoot. On the producer side, the exact renewed member set must still match current local public/disclosure/retention and anchor eligibility. A current receiver-owned eligibility summary bound to that exact member root and relevant local policy/incident versions can avoid repeating a whole scan; lost/dirty coverage requires funded revalidation or nondecision. Withdrawn/changed rows require successful funded DROP/replacement first, or the group is not renewed. One remaining public row cannot justify renewing unrelated withdrawn rows. The remote holder does not certify this native eligibility itself.

The holder pays for retaining the actual current count/bytes for the admitted extension; a checked cached aggregate is valid only under that exact authoritative membership root. For holder-local time t and admitted positive interval delta<=48 hours, the selected new deadline is `max(oldDeadline,t+delta)`, and must not exceed `t+48 hours`; a short request cannot shorten an existing lease. Charge the actual extension plus operation costs, not a fictional full new allocation or free renewed byte-time. The atomic commit advances stateVersion once. It cannot add forgotten rows, change roots, reset per-call history or multiply remaining duration by replay. A group with no surviving authoritative membership state returns UNKNOWN_GROUP/EXPIRED as appropriate; a fresh empty OPEN does not inherit the old membership root.

CLOSE checks the exact version/root, marks that group CLOSED, immediately removes its rows from **current** serving eligibility through the group-lifecycle gate, and schedules only locally funded incremental reclamation of its own rows. It does not delete shared native objects, another group's rows or the base public corpus. A bounded membership/GC cursor reclaims physical metadata while its charged allocation remains held. Every deletion names the exact old groupID and row/storage generation and conditionally deletes only if that same ownership/generation is still current. A delayed old-group sweep cannot delete a replacement row at the same cell/provider key. New row insertion and old deletion claims serialize through the authoritative writer, with claimed physical deletions fenced against reusing their generation. Snapshot-held old data are counted until their snapshot allocation expires; closing cannot release capacity still needed by live snapshots. There is no O(N)-row unmetered deletion hidden inside CLOSE.

Group expiry similarly makes rows ineligible at once through the current group gate. Late housekeeping cannot keep them current. Reclamation can lag but remains counted. A new OPEN receives a different groupID; delayed batches/renewals for the old group cannot bind the new one. An owner may stop serving even after an admission observation; this remains a scoped operational service, not permanent preservation.

## Snapshot, restart and concurrency semantics

ROUTE_LIST need not reveal a private group inventory or groupID. A current snapshot includes only rows whose individual lease or owning group is ACTIVE and unexpired at that exact snapshot cut. Its `[signedProviderAd,0]` rows retain the base historical `PRESENT_AT_NAMED_SNAPSHOT_CUT_ONLY` interpretation. Expiry/CLOSE/renewal after the cut cannot relabel that snapshot as current. New current snapshots evaluate the group lifecycle/deadline before including any row.

Simultaneous renewal and mutation serialize through expected stateVersion/memberRoot. A client normally serializes work per holder/group; stale concurrent work receives conflict and settles its actual costs. The server can reuse exact current aggregate summaries for renewal without scanning every member only because all admitted mutations maintain those summaries atomically. A corrupted/uncertain summary triggers nondecision/reconciliation, not an assumed zero-cost successful renewal.

The source persists the exact desired row set, last confirmed group version/root, submitted mutation call IDs and bounded expected before/after roots. Lost delivery is not failed computation: it first uses the ordinary retained action outcome where available. On a new authenticated group observation, an exact expected after-root/version can reconcile that one completed transition. If local history cannot establish which rows survive, it does not invent membership from a count or a familiar groupID; it reconstructs with explicitly bounded work or retires that group and opens/repopulates a new group under fresh authority. A receipt alone is not the source's local native acceptance.

A clean invocation-generation seal preserves the exact live group, deadline, membership and allocations without reissuing rows or funding. Actual holder/source restart applies the normal recovery fence to running jobs. Where monotonic lease continuity is uncertain, the group is not served as ACTIVE from old wall-clock guesses; fresh independently admitted renewal is permitted only if the holder still has coherent exact group membership and the local recovery policy explicitly authorizes it. Otherwise it expires and must be reopened/repopulated. A stale backup cannot recreate spent grant authority or forgotten membership by presenting an old root.

## Source publication state and explicit resources

For each exact target the source keeps `[scopeID,JudgmentID,desiredAdCID,phase,cellDepth,placementGroupRefs,ancestorMarkerProgress,lastAttemptReason]`. This is bounded publication bookkeeping, not an agent research assignment. It enumerates the actual fine scope and cannot omit required rows while declaring complete scope coverage.

Default fine-source allocation: 4096 aggregate targets, four selected replica placements per target, at most 16384 selected placement memberships and an additional at most 16384 live transient/partial/replacement memberships, at most 32768 actual provider-holder groups including empty/opening/retiring groups, 96 MiB total publication/group/receipt/work metadata, at most 32 pending batches and four concurrent paid route executors. Every partial shallow placement, uncertain-but-possibly-committed mutation and empty OPEN consumes these actual limits until coherently removed/expired/reconciled; it is not ignored because it is outside the desired four copies. More transient need causes defer/partial status before further allocation. Larger maximum-65536-target profiles must explicitly raise their corresponding membership/group and recurring resource allocations; changing only the target-count integer is not adequate.

The initial fine-holder example shares the base routing node's actual 65536-ad and 384-MiB routing store limits; it does not add another invisible copy. It permits at most 32768 group records, at most 65536 members in one group, and counts group metadata, live control authority and snapshot generations against its actual aggregate stores. If the host's general live-control limit is smaller than all admitted group/native obligations together, the group profile must supply a compatible larger allocation or decline; the 32768 group count is not a claim that the old entire control allocation can be spent twice.

The fine source's sample periodic envelope is 131072 service operations, 1 GiB application traffic and 3600 CPU-seconds per funded day, inclusive of this scope's grouping, placement, ordinary route lookup, markers, status and renewal work. These replace explicitly allocated shares of total node resources and are not automatically granted by a remote ad. The ordinary four-executor concurrency and per-call quotas remain. Any one dimension may prevent completion even when another has unused capacity.

Scope admission must reserve actual known selected/transient group stock, currently scheduled renewal occurrences, marker paths and an explicitly chosen initial-placement/exploration share against those aggregate envelopes. It cannot reuse the base 32768-call allowance and simultaneously promise a sparse 4096-target scope whose desired-group renewals already consume all 32768 calls. Before holders are known, admission uses conservative declared group ceilings or admits a provisional smaller preparation stage; as actual placement reveals active renewal groups and total charged group stock, the node narrows/degrades the scope or supplies an explicit larger allocation before claiming maintained coverage. A higher target limit by itself does not replenish periodic resources.

Membership selection/routing uses a persistent exact-target cursor. Candidate holders and a bounded batch queue group pending mutations by authenticated destination/group. If the queue is full, the source stops admitting more staged work and retains its exact next target; it does not manufacture one queue per destination. The queue is ordered by earliest existing lease renewal urgency, then admitted initial-placement age, then exact target/holder IDs, with one of every four eligible job slots reserved for initial placement while new targets remain. Native public-index growth cannot starve renewal indefinitely, and renewal does not intentionally consume all initial-placement opportunities; overload can still defeat either service bound and is reported.

Warm renewal contacts the already known actual group holder directly. It does not rerun every member's DHT lookup merely to extend an unchanged lease. A separate fair routing-refresh cursor revisits exact targets under its own counted jobs to detect changed nearest-holder opportunities and establish replacement placements before relinquishing old ones where capacity permits. An old group outside the reader's currently reached holder set can still store its rows but may no longer supply the conditional discoverability path. No status silently equates retained old placement with current routing convergence.

Markers for any deeply sharded fine key still use the base paid marker-path mechanism and their own finite leases. Group renewal does **not** renew ancestor markers. The source tracks and refreshes those exact paths separately. At the initial depth-zero cell no markers are needed; a rare exact key therefore normally avoids the cost of traversing the coarse foundation catalog, subject to actual route progress/admission.

## Cost and information boundaries

Let N be exact targets, r=4 selected copies, M distinct willing routing holders, G_active the active holder groups for one scope, G_total all charged group records/reservations across their lifecycles, L_i actual initial routing work for target i, and B actual bounded group batches. A complete chosen placement set requires at least rN retained provider memberships, before any partial/retiring extras. Its source accounting is Theta(N+rN), plus actual transient and empty-group state; the network cannot make a complete fine index appear from a 16-key coarse summary.

The initial service-operation cost includes `sum_i L_i`, group OPENs, B batch commits, and any deeper marker paths/retries. Batching lowers repeated per-call overhead where multiple mutations share a holder; it cannot merge unrelated remote authorities into one global transaction. A one-target new theorem still needs its own actual key placement and a receiving node still must check its proof.

One unchanged renewal pass uses one call per actually renewed active group, plus actual route/marker maintenance, rather than one call per row when many rows share holders. Closing/expired records consume capacity but are not automatically renewed. For the settled chosen placement set alone, with no extra/empty/retiring groups, its group count G_selected satisfies

`G_selected <= min(M,rN)`.

It can approach that upper bound. This is **not** a bound on all active groups during publication: for one target, three shallow copies followed by four deeper copies already permit seven holders. With R_active actual memberships in ACTIVE groups, including partial/replacement copies, and E_active empty ACTIVE groups, `G_active <= min(M,R_active + E_active)` for one scope; across S scopes replace M by SM. The uniqueness premise applies only to ACTIVE groups.

Total charged stock is instead `G_total = G_active + G_closed_or_expired_unreclaimed + G_opening_or_uncertain_reservations`, using disjoint accounting states and conservatively retaining uncertain allocations until reconciliation. G_total is bounded by the explicit 32768-group local cap, **not by M**: one holder can have a CLOSED old group awaiting reclamation and a new ACTIVE group for the same scope. Repeated lifecycle generations can leave more charged records until funded cleanup. Their metadata, snapshots and any retained rows remain charged independently. In a very large network with widely scattered keys, almost every active placement can land at a different holder, so group renewal may save little. It is not O(1) renewal per provider, and signatures do not make the control traffic free.

For the 4096-target example, rN=16384 chosen memberships/groups in the worst distinct-holder case. Two full renewals per day then use 32768 calls before routing, insertion or marker work. If all 32768 allowed charged groups happen to be ACTIVE and are renewed, this becomes 65536 calls; CLOSED/expired groups are not renewed, and unneeded empty/partial ACTIVE groups should instead be closed or allowed to expire through the counted policy. At the per-target 64-call route envelope, a cold first pass alone can require 262144 routing calls, already more than the sample single-day fine allowance. Thus the baseline does not claim same-day first publication in this worst case. Actual batching, cached close contacts and shorter convergent routes can help, but qualification must measure them. Until the scope is actually supplied and observed placed, it remains visibly PENDING/PARTIAL. Raising capacity, distributing index provision or narrowing the declared scope is a substantive operating choice, not a hidden theorem that a fixed laptop indexes every library instantly.

Conditional exact-query benefit is nevertheless specific. If J is in a successfully placed scope, the relevant markers/ads overlap the reader's stable reachable holder sets, the native provider remains willing, and the query has enough route/page budget, a reader who knows J reaches a provider through the exact key rather than probing P indistinguishable foundation catalogs. The cost depends on actual route work and the exact key's provider population, not all providers of the foundation. If millions of providers all offer J, provider selection can still be bounded because any one genuinely serving acceptable witness may suffice; not every provider needs to be enumerated for that use.

An unknown helpful lemma has no known J key. STRUCT1 keys, checked adapters, local learned candidate ranking and evidence of actual useful applications remain necessary. This exact lane must not be advertised as a solution to semantic relevance, a guarantee of every contribution's eventual use, or evidence of exponential knowledge production.

## Required paper and future qualification cases

- Identical coarse catalogs across P honest providers: an exact-J membership avoids the forced P-provider scan under stated route conditions; outside the declared scope the same lower bound remains visible.
- The public library contains more judgments than the selected fine scope: report exact intended/placed/uncovered counts, not FULL_EXACT for the whole library.
- 64 large ads exceed the 64-KiB control body: reject/split the new request without partial hidden application or an oversized allocation.
- One invalid row or stale expected CID in a batch: no row changes; the whole paid call has a bounded failure result.
- Group renewal races a batch: exactly one expected-version transition wins; the other reconciles through a fresh paid operation without losing committed rows.
- A batch commits and its reply is lost: do not replay side effects or assume absence; reconcile the exact call/expected root or repopulate safely.
- A group expires between snapshot creation and delivery: returned historical rows do not claim a current lease or native service.
- Group CLOSE is requested for 65536 rows: current eligibility ends through one authoritative gate; metered reclamation and held snapshots prevent uncharged instant deletion or premature capacity reuse.
- A fresh group is opened after an old group is forgotten: empty means empty; the old membership root/receipt cannot magically restore rows.
- A fine exact key uses deeper shards: renewing its leaf group alone is insufficient; expired ancestor markers make the route visibly incomplete.
- The settled active group count approaches rN: report the large actual renewal cost rather than claiming the group optimization is always effective; separately count partial/retiring/empty and uncertain group stock.
- A base PUT/DROP targets a group-owned row: return ROW_OWNER_CONFLICT; no row can disappear while the owning group's exact root/aggregate/version remains unchanged.
- The native checker is suspended: routing entries may become stale leads, while actual local/public native reliance blocks; route signatures cannot repair the basis.

This is a concrete index-supply contract with finite defaults and explicit service costs. Its success criterion is demonstrated unfamiliar exact-query acquisition and checked application under declared load, not merely safe refusal when its selected scope cannot be maintained.
