## What this can change

A tiny run can answer whether the isolated fixed-basis improvement survives real
whole-operation overhead and whether cuts are competitive with ordinary reuse
on this actual small theory. It cannot measure a large-network crossover, prove
universal speedup or qualify a public accelerator.

Recommended prospective decision margin: at least 20% reduction in the two-cut
inclusive CPU, with no wall/memory regression that defeats practical use. This
is an engineering margin, not statistical significance; root must adopt it before
launch. Also inspect standalone cost including new descriptor capture/migration.
A mere reduction in `kernel_calls` is not the performance gate. Expected counts
481/490 versus 25/34 are useful implementation checks, not cost measurements.

- Correctness/closure failure: no performance adoption; preserve the failure.
- No material old-to-new improvement: shelve this optimization investment.
- Material improvement, but ordinary sequential/batch clearly wins after relevant
  setup: retain ordinary as default; no public cut productization on this evidence.
- Competitive restored cuts: retain the narrow optional candidate for a separate
  release decision, with explicit user workload and measured cost account.
- Close/noisy result: inconclusive; do not start repetitions automatically.

One small-root point cannot estimate an ancestral-size slope or prove eventual
break-even. The singleton's source-level lack of a growing theorem-proof replay
term remains a separate conditional locality argument. A plausible abstract
crossover is not enough to justify more engineering without a real user workload.
No result here establishes new mathematics or final-v5 completion.
