## What changed and why it is safe

A dedicated inert host can now initialize once and append separately selected
bundles without rebuilding a complete generation. Its stable descriptor binds
profile, semantic namespace, fixed quotas and explicit partial-publication policy;
it does not identify current contents. Snapshot and counts describe current data
separately. Existing static prepare/serve remains supported.

Under one exclusive host lock, each append commits evidence and locators, then
resolved previews, then index records. Thus every committed prefix preserves
index-to-custody support. The operator explicitly approves partial disclosure of
all supplied data. After failed append cleanup, service may expose that committed
prefix without asserting batch completion. This avoids a whole-batch recovery
protocol. The argument assumes protected local stores, serialized supported
writers and SQLite durability; see [contract](CONTRACT.md) and the
[invariant](../ordinary_connect_ownership/INCREMENTAL-HOST-INVARIANT.md).

At most 64 targets are accepted per append; cumulative targets use fixed store
quotas. Normal append does not require a scan of all historical evidence. Supplied
duplicate bytes still cost validation, and producer export is not made incremental
by this host change. Existing evolving-owner publication and static delta shards
remain legitimate alternatives; see the [fair comparison](../ordinary_connect_ownership/HOSTING-PAYOFF-CHALLENGE.md).

## Evidence and scope

Nine focused control groups passed, including cumulative 64+1 targets, duplicate
idempotence, capacity refusals and orderly exceptions after real commits. The
public init -> append -> append -> serve lifecycle returned both exact retained
contributions through four typed reads; every process closed naturally. The
stable descriptor remained unchanged while current snapshots advanced. No owner
or mathematical worker was opened.

The archive and all extracted bytes were independently checked. No new relocated
preparer execution was performed: unchanged behavior evidence was reused, and
the updated compatibility pins were checked against the new package.

Assembly, controls, public lifecycle and packing used 18.984375 known CPU seconds
in total, excluding development/review/model effort. Overlapping wall durations
are not summed. No runtime remains active. No rerun is required for adoption.

This establishes incremental inert hosting on the tested local profile, not
actual process-kill/power-loss recovery, independent-host operation, causal
growth acceleration, a producer scaling improvement or final worldwide release.
Digests identify approved local bytes; they do not establish an external publisher.
