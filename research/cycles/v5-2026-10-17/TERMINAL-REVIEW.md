## Early architectural disposition

Root's selected route is a **closed two-form evidence profile and custody
store**: the existing exact mixed schema or a distinct terminal schema. It
reuses existing opaque packet HTTP routes and one prospective search handoff,
acquisition and original budget. A separate terminal store/mode was considered;
root rejected its public-operational bifurcation because a single unknown-ID
target may have either evidence form, and discovering this should not force a
new query/handoff. No generic plugin registry, new mathematical checker, global
ledger, wrapper theorem or provider-issued mathematical authority is needed.

The envelope is proposed as canonical inert data containing schema, exact192-byte
aggregate headerH, exact184-byte module journalJ, member index, ordered sibling
digests and receipt bytes. The wire/custody author will fix the exact format and
caps. Structural parsing calls existing header/profile/member binding. Actual
acceptance calls existing `AggregateReceiptVerifier.receive` and `select_member`
through the ordinary client's direct admission core. The result is the existing
live `A-member` warrant, not a new authority class or reconstructed handle.

This architecture is **supported in principle**, pending the complete concrete
wire/client contract and source mapping. Simpler direct admission alone cannot
supply public custody/discovery/acquisition. The new closed inspector must
dispatch on exact schema and then call the unchanged mixed parser or the new
terminal parser. It must not relax mixed semantics, guess a proof form after
failure, or accept a provider-advertised tag as proof of the downloaded format.
Concrete constructor/type/profile overrides are necessary at existing exact
class gates; monkeypatching frozen module globals is not an alternative.

## Exact preservation: three identities must remain distinct

Let `decode(J)` determine selected relation imageI, foundationF, policyP,
module summaryS, ModuleIDM and dependency rank. Let `E` be a terminal envelope,
and `D=SHA256(E)` its opaque custody identity.

1. **Mathematical/interface identity:** `M = summary_id(S)`. Existing declaration
   ExportIDs and formal index features continue to derive from authenticated
   group openings ofM. Replacing evidence must not rewrite their namespace,
   summary, names or declaration interfaces.
2. **Exact warrant identity:**J. Different validJ values may describe the sameM,
   for example at different ranks. If an existing import or comparator selects
   `J*`, acceptance must require `J=J*`, not merely the sameM. Unknown-ID discovery
   can initially choose anM; after the envelope is selected, its exactJ must be
   exposed and bound by the live warrant. The proposed optional
   `expected_exact_journal` is an appropriate explicit constraint.
3. **Evidence representation identity:**D and exact envelope length. A different
   aggregate certificate or member opening may certify the sameJ. Such an
   alternate representation is not new mathematics; remote offers/readback
   must still bind their exact requestedD/length/provider/profile.

The fixed-target growth gate requires **preseal targetJ = postseal terminalJ**
and matchingM, not merely a discoverable theorem with a similar statement.
Cold acceptance must not require the target's old native witness. The retained
definition/group/body commitments do not disappear with that witness.

## Acceptance argument and trust boundaries

For locally adopted A/I/F/P, successful terminal use must factor as follows:

    exact envelope bytes and finite parse
      -> structurally bound (H,J,index,path) under adopted A/I/F/P
      -> genuine live A receipt acceptance for exact H
      -> genuine member selection for exact J/index/path
      -> exact selected export opening under J's module summary
      -> ordinary demanded semantics and atomic import
      -> new independently checked contribution.

Only the genuine receipt/member and subsequent checking steps supply authority.
Store/index insertion, a valid Merkle path to an unprovedH, downloaded success
JSON, exact packet digest or a popularity score does not. Reuse the existing
same-owner receipt cache; membership selection still binds every requestedJ,
index and ordered path. The authority implication inherits the actual aggregate
relation/checker/cryptographic assumptions; this route neither strengthens those
assumptions into a theorem nor introduces a weaker provider-trust premise.

After network/structural retrieval, opening selection and custody readback must
be followed by live issuer revalidation before returning the warrant. An error,
expiry or owner substitution invokes the existing fail-stop original-owner
cleanup. No token, cache, database record or copied description restores an old
issuer after reopening. Existing last-delta APIs retain their old contract.

## Budget and lifecycle requirements

- A selected operation has one search handoff, one original acquisition budget
  and deadline, and one fixed closed two-form profile. Its selected evidence
  representation may be mixed or terminal, but choosing another offered form
  must not consume the handoff twice or obtain new endpoints/allowances. This
  is not an automatic retry policy and does not waive fail-stop refusals.
- Terminal network requests, export/body downloads, bytes, failed requests and
  A receipt verification spend the same existing budget as the operation's
  earlier search/location reservations. New storage shares the original finite
  workspace/output census. Separate caps are nested constraints, not fresh funds.
- The client must not call a decorated public admission action inside its
  decorated acquisition action. Use one reviewed private/unwrapped direct-A
  core with one action boundary and exactly one verification reservation before
  actual issuer work. Structural acquisition itself must not also reserve that
  verification or invoke another verifier.
- Direct admission and acquired admission should reuse the same exact receipt
  cache, preserving its A/profile/H/receipt hash-and-length key. A cache hit may
  avoid cryptographic verification, not submitted-byte, membership or controller
  charges. Cold/new-owner requests cannot use persisted authority.
- Closing a structural terminal acquisition must not accidentally transfer or
  strand independent issuer ownership; the ordinary client owns the original
  issuers and closes them explicitly. Public source/origin pins must include all
  new modules used by dynamic or inherited methods.

## Availability is evidence plus needed semantics, not a provider flag

A terminal receipt proves committed mathematics but supplies neither declaration
openings nor transparent bodies. Publication therefore needs explicit authentic
semantic material for exported declarations. Existing `ModuleStore` and formal
index insertion can serve those components without a new semantic format.

An acquired export opening plus caller-selected bodies establishes only that
selected material's present custody. Further atomic-import demands may require
other groups, bodies or external modules. Missing material must return an
explicit unmet demand/refusal; it cannot be replaced by an invented opaque
constant, stale grant or claim that all future semantics are available. All
additional acquisition stays under the original finite allowance.

Onward terminal dissemination can preserve the same envelope and semantic
components after the original supplier disappears, but independent custody,
funding and correlated failure assumptions remain distinct. A receipt cannot
repair lost bytes. The eventual real integration should select the needed
semantic closure in advance, use a provider-independent custodian, and consume
the resulting material through ordinary source authoring, without private state
promotion. No extra transport demonstration is needed before that combined gate.

## Growth consequence: restores a missing premise, not automatic benefit

Let `C_native(i,J)` be requesti's full cost for the exact native targetJ under
the equally optimized unsealed route, and `C_terminal(i,J)` its full cost under
the new terminal route. Both include actual search, offer/transfer, structural
parsing, verification, needed semantics, controller work and applicable cache
state. LetK include incremental seal production, publication, custody/repair
and failed acquisition costs not already allocated to the requests.

For an explicitly chosen common resource measure, sharing the sealed target is
net beneficial precisely in the conditional region

    sum_i [C_native(i,J) - C_terminal(i,J)] > K.

A justified uniform margin `d>0` yields the sufficient threshold `N*d>K`.
For CPU/storage/bandwidth vectors, retain components or specify nonnegative
weights before adding them. Never assign a future unmeasured receipt size or
proving cost a favorable value merely because the route exists.

This route removes the **mandatory target-delta witness** term from terminal
receiving. It still pays for the actual aggregate receipt, membership opening,
interface and demanded semantics. Receipt wire/controller costs may outweigh
the removed work for small targets; no universal per-request improvement follows.
The meaningful repeated-use population is fresh/cold or new-owner admissions
under a fair cache comparator. Counting every warm local theorem use as another
avoided native check would artificially handicap the baseline, whose existing
live warrants and optimized caches already avoid that work.

The prior fixed-target growth formula can therefore be applied only after this
target-preserving route is implemented and its cost assumptions are supplied.
The addition is consequential because it makes the modeled post-seal choice
available, not because an envelope alone establishes useful or exponential
mathematical growth. Further source-map/minimum-control disposition follows the
authors' concrete contract.

## Publication/recovery ambiguity resolved in principle

Review of frozen `_publish/reconcile` found a consequential difference: mixed
packets carry semantic material from which replay can reconstruct publication;
a terminal envelope does not. A new `publish_terminal(raw, detached_material)`
could partially fail before all supplied material reached stores. Inheriting
old replay, which reloads only the packet, would then silently lose the missing
publication inputs unless a new contract required resupply or durable retention.

Root chooses the simpler resolution: **retain semantics first, publish second**.
A structural `retain_semantics` call stores and reads back exact bounded group
openings and permitted bodies in the existing immutable `ModuleStore`; it can
fail partially and grants neither publication success nor mathematical authority.
`publish(raw, export_names)` then requires all selected export groups already
retained and authentic under the terminal's exactM/summary before creating its
publication intent. It does not take detached pending semantic arrays.

This supports ordinary recovery without a new sidecar or another service:
the intent binds envelope and selected names; the immutable retained groups
provide reconstructible inputs under the same exclusive workspace ownership.
Reconciliation re-reads these same selected groups by module/name, not the full
store, and refuses absent/corrupt material rather than silently dropping it.
No future-body availability is promised; missing bodies remain explicit, and
the exact fixed commitments identify any subsequently supplied body.

Source review must verify these ordering and readback requirements. A publication
or reconciliation result is still structural custody/index state, never proof.
