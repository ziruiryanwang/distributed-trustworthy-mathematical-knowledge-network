## What growth is justified by this decision

The existing conditional method/bridge and demand-overlap arguments remain the
theory; no new theorem-count proxy or inevitable exponential-growth claim is added.
This investigation identifies concrete mechanisms that affect their premises:

- Preserving native APIs avoids requiring downstream agents to reconstruct
  instance search, notation and domain automation before useful application.
- Keeping small inert adoption optional expands the permitted reuse routes where
  executing an unfamiliar publisher's tooling is unacceptable.
- Reusing actual upstream validation code can reduce the maintenance and
  compatibility cost of making those routes dependable.

These are architectural enabling/efficiency claims with source-backed mechanisms,
not measured uptake. The same adapters may benefit ordinary repositories and a
decentralized provider network. That is public value, not a reason to demand an
exclusive network advantage. Useful growth still requires successful downstream
applications whose gains cover acquisition, assurance, adaptation, failed search
and sustained service. A popular result, valid proof or passing fixture alone
does not establish that comparison.

The immediate product implication is substantive: stop optimizing an obligatory
custom ecosystem; make a smaller interoperability/assurance layer that respects
the libraries people actually use. Preserve strict acceptance and honest finite
costs while allowing different receiver policies explicitly. The full deployable
v5 goal is unchanged and not complete.
