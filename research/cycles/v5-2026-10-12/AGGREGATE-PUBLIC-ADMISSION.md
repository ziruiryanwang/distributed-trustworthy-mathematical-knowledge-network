### Exact refusal and revocation policy

- An expired, closed, changed-profile or over-controller-CPU owner revokes its
  own aggregate/member registries whenever `_active` detects that condition.
  Loaded-source or installation-pin failure in `_check_live` also revokes.
- While the owner is otherwise live, malformed receive arguments or exhausted
  receive/output allowance **before reservation** refuse without changing old
  handles or reserving a new operation. Concurrency refusal does not close an
  operation already in progress.
- Once `receive` reserves its operation, receipt bytes, CPU and output envelope,
  **every later failure revokes all of that owner's aggregate/member handles**.
  Reservations remain charged; evidence is not erased. This includes a malformed
  full-length header, failed path/preflight, unsuccessful worker or finalization.
- Every admitted member API attempt consumes one call before its live-pin work;
  submitted well-shaped member selections and encoded openings consume bytes
  before cache/use decisions. Wrong membership, foreign/fabricated handles,
  invalid group openings and member call/storage/byte caps refuse without a new
  handle, but do not alone revoke previously live aggregates. Charged failed
  calls/bytes are not refunded. Final liveness/pin failure does revoke.
- `close` revokes the owner and all descendants. `report` is only local accounting,
  does not mint or validate authority, and is not a liveness/cryptographic proof.

Revocation is a local capability-lifetime rule, not a statement that previously
proved mathematics has become false. Python internals and controller installation
are trusted; these checks are not an arbitrary-code or concurrent-mutation
security sandbox. The initial API does not cache receipt verification calls.
Multiple selected members reuse one still-live aggregate handle; duplicate exact
member selection returns its stored member only after charging and rechecking.

### Current API cost includes full installation rehashes

Do **not** describe a public `select_member`, `describe_member` or `open_group`
call as merely `O(log member_count)`. `_member_begin -> _check_live` rereads and
hashes the entire pinned executable/runtime/source closure; successful calls
normally perform a second final `_check_live`. If its total bytes are L and the
pin count is p, each live-pin phase includes O(L+p) reads/hashing, plus loaded
module path resolution. The small Merkle-path computation is an additional
term. Receipt admission similarly has three live-install checks in the normal
path. No timing advantage is claimed for these unrun sources.

L belongs to the adopted local checking installation, not to the global knowledge
corpus or ancestral theorem proofs. This preserves proof-history locality but can
still be a substantial per-call constant. Keep mutation revocation now. Any
future amortization needs an explicit loaded-code/installation-lifetime model and
fresh review; timestamps/stat-cache shortcuts cannot silently replace these pins.

## Three identities and two different proof objects

Let M be a semantic ModuleID, J an exact184-byte module journal, and H an
exact192-byte V2 aggregate header. H names A, the fixed module-relation image I,
foundation F, semantic policy P, a member root/count, maximum mathematical rank
and a separate aggregate seal height.

- A legacy receipt proves the application claim `(I,J)` directly.
- An aggregate receipt proves `(A,H)`. A member opening binds J to H's member set.
- Together the second pair establishes the mathematics warranted by J. It does
  **not** manufacture an SDK receipt whose application claim is `(I,J)`.

J is an existential checked warrant, not a unique proof-history identifier.
Different complete derivations can establish the same J. Different ranks give
different Js even if M is identical. M determines semantic storage/deduplication;
J determines exact old child premises; `(A,H)` determines aggregate receipt trust.

## Same-call receipt admission invariants

2. Receipt bytes, H, count/path bounds and resource limits are checked and charged
   before deduplication or expensive work. H must bind the adopted A/I/F/P and
   exact V2 profile. H is an expected statement, not evidence of its own truth.
3. A fresh supervised same-call `verify A receipt H` succeeds with clean process

   pass before promotion. No handle is minted first and finalized afterward.
6. Selected-member promotion separately binds exact J, index, count and sibling
   path to that live verified H. It uses the V2 domain separators, exact path

## Why recursive mixed I/A trust is sufficient

The existing aggregate relation allows exact assumptions `(I,J)` and `(A,H_old)`.
For I it requires exact J equality. For A it validates an exact selected member
opening and binds `H_old.A=A`. Every unique declared assumption must be used.
The reused guest must call `env::verify` for every returned assumption; production
must resolve all genuine assumptions before a final receipt can be admitted.

There are two induction measures, with different meanings:

1. **Seal height:** the new aggregate height is one plus the maximum imported
   aggregate height; header height is positive and overflow is refused. Thus
   aggregate premises have strictly smaller seal height. I premises are terminals
   for this induction.
2. **Mathematical rank:** every newly checked native J has rank one plus the
   maximum exact direct-parent rank. Thus its native edges descend strictly.

Assume the adopted module checker correctly establishes conditional Lean-policy
extensions; the actual recursive proof system is sound for this branching I/A
composition; the selected image-to-checker correspondence is correct; and the
hash commitments bind. Induct on seal height for aggregate premises, then on
native rank/topological order for new witnesses. Each selected member J is a
valid warrant under F/P. Receipt verification plus membership therefore supports
local import without downloading the proof bodies behind prior aggregate seals.

The receiver need not traverse this induction as data: full verification of the
resolved final receipt enforces the recursive claim. The induction is the
architecture's assurance argument. A proof-system certificate of a buggy checker
remains a certificate of a buggy checker; this seam does not remove the existing
checker/refinement/security-update obligations.

## Resource/locality consequences and honest limits

For q_I selected distinct I receipts, q_A selected distinct A receipts, f_A A
member openings and k new unsealed witnesses, the receiving work has the form

    q_I * VerifyI + q_A * VerifyA
    + O(f_A * log(member_count))
    + sum(CheckNative(new_unsealed_witness))
    + selected_interface/body acquisition and reconstruction
    + repeated O(L+p) local installation pin checks
    + bounded controller/transport/copy overhead.

There is no mandatory term for theorem-proof steps behind the selected A seals.
Here member_count is at most48, and the unsealed packet has explicit finite caps.
This does not claim constant time in selected interfaces, mathematical computation,
proof size, failed searches or required transparent semantics. Source/SDK analysis
and later bounded measurements must justify actual receipt-verification constants.

All submitted variants and duplicate calls stay charged before storage choices.
Track receipt claims, exact warrants, semantic modules, openings and bytes
separately. Neither warrant count, seal height nor maximum rank measures useful
growth. Sealing costs are amortizable investments, not free work: a full-cost
comparison still needs producer cost, failed attempts, retained interfaces,
custody/repair and realistic downstream reuse.

Availability must retain the genuine A receipt, H, selected J/member opening,
semantic group openings and demanded body material. A proof certificate does not
restore vanished definitions or regenerate an unavailable opening from its root.
Custody and indexes remain untrusted for mathematical validity but essential for
discovering and obtaining these objects. Operational outages/refusals are not
counterexamples to the theorem being valid.
