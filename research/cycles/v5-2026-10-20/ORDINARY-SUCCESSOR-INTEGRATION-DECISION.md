## Public semantic continuation: what already works and what is missing

The installed `run` command already accepts explicitly trusted local owner
code plus finite parameters and `runtime_mode`. It substitutes exact
`{"$installed":"runtime_adoption"}` and `{"$installed":"foundation"}` roles.
An owner can open one validated workspace/client and perform this sequence:

1. Bind all explicitly authorized contacts (including alternative supplier Q)
   before the one-shot search handoff, or use the existing located workflow.
2. Discover/resolve a candidate and choose an offered packet using existing
   bounded APIs; construct `SemanticReader(client, candidate, limits=...)`.
3. `stage(candidate, entry)` then `preview()`. Local policy explicitly chooses
   demanded internal member journals, additional offered evidence, declaration
   suppliers and body suppliers. There is no system-selected research agenda.
4. For one body, call `try_fetch_body(demand, max_bytes=..., supplier="P",
   attempt_timeout_ms=...)`. A settled unavailable response leaves the same
   helper/client live; caller may explicitly choose authorized Q with the same
   API. All calls share the original counters/deadline and failed-attempt charge.
5. Once structurally staged, `finish({packet_module: admission_limits, ...})`
   performs actual existing admission/import; use its live context in that
   same owner operation before closing. Preview/data/JSON reports are not warrants.

The lower-level `client.try_acquire_body(...)` is also available without the
helper. Old fail-stop body APIs remain unchanged. No new contact may be invented
after failure, no renewal/refund is implied, and no persisted handle can resume
mathematical authority after the process closes.

The minimal usability work is **documentation and an ordinary example owner**:
show helper limits, exact member/packet choices, authorized supplier labels,
per-attempt time/byte caps, finite caller loop/stop rule, admission limits and
use of the returned context. Do not reuse `semantic_check.py` or `split_check.py`
as product owners: they contain private fixture seeding and workload constants.

By contrast, built-in `reader` currently requires only query/contact/selection
arguments, does one admission/import, and returns NEED_SEMANTICS when additional
structure is needed. It never calls the helper and then closes the client. If
a later built-in semantic reader is desired, it needs explicit helper limits,
bounded action/member/supplier policy and an action-before-admission branch.
Blindly appending the helper after the old reader risks duplicate admission and
closes handles too early. Do not add a large JSON workflow language merely to
avoid the already supported local agent/owner API.
