## New concrete consequence: certification is not what bounds this family

Let B be the genuine BASE I frontier, M the proposed evaluator method directly
over B, and A_i an application depending only on M and B. Before certifying M,
the ordinary selected closure for A_i has B plus at most two native witnesses:
M and A_i. After certifying M it has at most one native witness A_i above that
frontier (and any separately demanded BASE semantics/warrant). Both bounds are
independent of the number of other applications in the global corpus.

This follows from exact backward-closure serialization in
`MixedImportSession.onward_packet`; unrelated A_j are not ancestors. It assumes
the stated dependency shape, bounded per-application proof/parameters/semantics,
available compatible evidence and existing caps. Arbitrary chains, branching
additional premises, or giant equation proofs do not inherit this bound.

Therefore certifying M buys avoidance of its repeated otherwise-uncached native
check/acquisition; it does **not** turn this family from unbounded into bounded.
Keeping M native can be the better lifetime route. A receiver cache reduces the
eligible savings further. An optimized ordinary shared library can also reuse
the same universal theorem, so mathematical abstraction is not itself a
cryptographic/network advantage. No positive current lifetime margin follows.

The method nevertheless has genuine value: it centralizes structural induction
and allows later interpretation/transport proofs to instantiate it. Equation
proofs and required semantics remain charged work, never hidden behind the
word "universal". Independent local choices need not follow this pattern.
