## Why existing public calls do not generally suffice

The reviewer read `direct_contacts.bind`, the existing candidate and exact-name/
body public methods, and the source-derived next-central decision.

`resolve(provider, record)` requires the exact record to have appeared in the
current search, but that membership check is not provider-specific. Thus
`resolve(Q, record_returned_from_P)` already works if Q holds the exact indexed
group. It does not manufacture a search record for a previously unseen base
declaration. `resolve_declaration(origin_candidate, ...)` inherits the immutable
provider stored in that origin candidate. Q may hold only a needed body or
declaration and need not have a matching index hit from which to obtain a
separate origin candidate.

The lower acquisition stack already accepts a provider argument and lends all
owner-selected contacts under the same acquisition budget. Therefore a small
public/helper successor can expose an existing capability; it needs neither
a duplicate transport nor a new discovery or authority protocol.

- The helper remains finite and caller-directed. No automatic supplier
  routing, retry, proof-form fallback, failure recovery, grant renewal or new
  global research task list is introduced.
- New controls exercise only changed authorization/origin/cache/budget
  behavior. Actual success with two endpoints alone is insufficient: a later
  retained-data test must establish that P genuinely lacks the demanded
  body and Q supplies that exact commitment.

## Conditional supplier-independence argument

For fixed mathematical address and commitment `a`, let `Validate(a, b)` be
the unchanged structural validation of returned bytes. Replacing supplier P
with an authorized Q must preserve `a` and validate Q's bytes with the same
predicate. Actual acceptance subsequently runs the unchanged live checker
over the selected authenticated mathematical data. Supplier identity can
affect availability, latency and resource charges, but must not change the
mathematical acceptance rule.

This does not establish that Q is reachable, that the system can discover Q,
that repeated failures recover, or that available contacts are independent.
The existing hash/checker/runtime assumptions remain; changing contact is not
a new proof.
