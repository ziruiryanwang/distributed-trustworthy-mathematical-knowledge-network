## Consequential source argument

Let L be the existing canonical compiled feature set, and F the optional plan.
The unchanged compiler first validates the registered immutable profile; the new
planner then retains every member of L and adds eligible registered pair keys in
profile order until the existing eight-key wire bound is reached. It does not
change feature extraction, index materialization or mathematical acceptance.

Thus L is a subset of F. Every old rendezvous route obtained from a subset of L
is still obtainable from F. An eligible pair is the conjunction of two already
required atomic features, so adding it does not change the exact predicate when
extraction is correct. For each committed index partition, choosing a minimum
posting count over the expanded choices cannot increase that minimum. These
facts support better anchor selectivity without inventing a new index scheme.

They do not prove universal end-to-end improvement. Extra features increase
summary/validation bytes and can cause refusal under an unchanged aggregate
budget even when the legacy request could complete. No fallback, refund,
completeness beyond committed coverage, semantic-equivalence oracle or inevitable
useful-growth claim follows. The natural hot-first-pair/rare-second-pair corpus is
a focused structural discriminator, not a mathematical usefulness benchmark.

The direct and located entry points add keyword-only `plan_pairs=False`, reject
non-boolean values before allocation, and compute the opted-in plan before
session allocation. The default compiler path and default report shape remain
unchanged. Query features remain exactly bound to their generation/session and
existing envelope. Plan reports are copied independently into the generation
record and returned result, including the located no-contact outcome. No owner,
route authority, contact map, budget, grant, fingerprint or checking policy is
changed. There is no blocking source defect in these three successors.
