## Resource claim boundary

Preserve full public search-generation/acquisition HTTP counters and the
provider status (request, reply, header and failure data), separate from
prepaid authorization bytes. Save final bounded on-disk census for both arms.
The exact packet/group payload and finite service duration provide a limited
publication payload byte-time upper bound. Total database/temporary-file
byte-time is not measured, and postrun frozen research evidence remains stored.
Do not describe final file bytes as peak storage or total byte-time.

Existing output reservations do not supply a missing physical byte-time bound:
the client directory census runs at action boundaries and reports
`hard_disk_limit=False`; the supervisor's periodic log limits are not a disk
quota. SQLite page caps bound principal databases, not automatically rollback
journals, temporary worker output or transient writes. In particular,
128 MiB multiplied by the 230-second child horizon is not a justified upper
bound for the complete arm's physical storage-byte-time. No new telemetry or
experiment is added to answer this source-level accounting question.

The result can support or reject an observed **full-workflow CPU** margin with
other resource coordinates disclosed. It does not automatically support Pareto
dominance, an all-resource scalar benefit, or a positive unknown-price region.
Any stronger total-resource claim needs actual additional bounds/prices; it is
not inferred from a CPU win. No positive sign, feasible n>1 population, global
discovery completeness, exponential growth or certificate payback is assumed.

The producer's common pre-existing M creation is present in both histories
and cancels only under that named initial-state comparison. Speculative
