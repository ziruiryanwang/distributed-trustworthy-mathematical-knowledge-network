## Decision

**The measured general-zkVM certificate is much too expensive to justify making
this route mandatory for every small contribution. This is not evidence that
decentralized trustworthy sharing is impractical.** Keep ordinary checked sharing
and selectively supplied succinct frontiers distinct. A consumer can request or
fund certification, but moving the payer does not remove its physical cost.

The highest-payoff architectural change is to make the evidence/acceptance
contract explicitly consumer-relative and keep certification off the mandatory
publication path. This largely uses existing O/C mechanisms, not a new network,
scheduler or economic ledger. For genuinely cheaper succinct production, investigate
a different *judgment-witness representation* before replacing cryptographic
backends. Repeating the already failed restricted-checker optimization is not that
change. The three ranked recommendations are at the end.

Strict no-ancestral-proof consumers still require a grounded available certified
cut (or another actually supported equally sound bounded witness). When none
exists, defer/request a certificate or return a typed refusal. Do not quietly
substitute unbounded proof replay and call it the same scalable guarantee.

### Later user clarification: practical compromises are permitted

Root relayed the user's latest explicit criterion: permit compromises that make
the software genuinely usable and beneficial to humanity. The cost evidence
does **not** warrant requiring a succinct certificate for every arbitrary cold
first use, nor does it warrant changing mathematical soundness. A practical
default can therefore be a **bounded ordinary library lane with protected local
amortization**, with a separately requested strict no-history/thin-client lane.
The former may check a finite relevant dependency closure once and retain a
grounded local result; it must report and cap that work. The latter may wait or
refuse when no qualifying cut/short proof exists. No operation needs to scan the
global corpus, but an otherwise-uncached selected dependency closure can still
be large; those are different complexity claims.

This is an explicit tradeoff in *cold latency, evidence size and supported
delivery service*, not in truth or reliance on arbitrary peers. It is not a
proof that all useful workloads will fit the ordinary lane. Under this updated
user criterion, rank a usable sound O path and cross-session local amortization
ahead of universal first-use succinctness. Keep certificate production available
as selective shared infrastructure rather than the price of joining or publishing.

## Existing L/O/C portfolio, not a new invention

The authoritative definitions are in
`../v5-2026-10-20/TYPED-RENEWAL-NEXT-PRIORITY.md`:

| Lane | Meaning | Distinct obligation |
|---|---|---|
| L | Named optimized isolated/local policy | Same initial mathematics, local tools, representation and caches; no free donation of genuinely new remote discoveries |
| O | Ordinary decentralized sharing with actual local checking | Every omitted premise must already be grounded; bounded native suffixes may reuse certified cuts |
| C | Optional succinct-certified sharing | Actual accepted certificate/semantic policy and available openings; production and maintenance are charged |

O is already part of the intended software, not a competitor that v5 must defeat
on every contribution. Existing mixed packets, native checking, I/A receipts,
exact-J frontier replacement, interface-only imports, semantic custody and
publication supply substantial pieces. `prepare_mixed_seal` already selects
exact backward closures and encodes aggregate preparation. Reimplementing those
as a “consumer-driven verifier” or a second frontier planner is wasted work.

The retained matched sharing case reports L=76.46875 and O=44.953125 known CPU
seconds, a 31.515625 difference at the same canonical READY mathematics and named
initial state. It supports one ordinary-sharing comparison, not certificate
profitability. It concerns another workload; do not use that difference as U's
certificate saving per recipient.

For a chosen target and service horizon, the separate certificate question is:

\[
\sum_{i\in E}\Delta_i > P + H.
\]

Here E is the set of *eligible otherwise-uncached* uses, Δ_i is the actual or
justifiably bounded O-minus-C full per-use saving after equal optimizations,
P is incremental certificate production/adoption/failed-work cost, and H is
incremental finite-horizon service/retention/repair cost. Shared work cancels only
where actually equal. Negative margins and failed discovery remain charged.
For future action, sunk artifacts can be initial state; for a from-genesis
benefit claim, their production cannot disappear. This restates the existing
decision boundary only to apply the new measured U production, not to propose
another calculator.

Sensitivity to an **assumed net CPU saving per eligible cold use**, ignoring
additional fixed/service costs, is illustrative:

## What consumer-driven certification changes

The safe protocol distinction is between **mathematical claim**, **available
evidence**, **local acceptance**, and **published availability offer**. A producer
can share an ordinary proof object without first buying a succinct receipt. A
consumer supplies a profile and finite budget, then selects already supported
evidence: current grounded cache, bounded ordinary witness, or genuine certificate.
An unknown proof object is candidate material until locally admitted; sharing it
does not turn metadata, a peer signature or previous local success into authority.

For the strict receiver profile, require a supplied cut intersecting every path
to omitted opaque-proof ancestry. If no such available cut exists, return
`NEED_CERTIFICATE`/unsupported evidence rather than traverse indefinitely. A
separate explicitly selected O profile may do bounded direct work and return an
honest resource refusal. These outcomes must not be hidden behind one success flag.

A consumer, custodian, contributor or independent specialist may produce the
same public warrant under an adopted policy. Production is untrusted computation;
its output is independently verified. This removes an obligation from every
contributor and permits amortization, but the network still needs someone to
pay the actual production and retain its inputs/results. On-demand certification
also adds first-use latency. Without a funded service bound, it is not an immediate
availability promise, and requester identities must not be able to force unlimited
unpaid work. The separate demand-protocol audit owns those resource details.

## Alternative mechanisms: what actually changes

### Native proof objects, logical compression and reflection

The most economical certificate can be an ordinary short mathematical proof.
Use shared proof DAGs, genuinely general lemmas, checked rewriting/decision
certificates, and proof by reflection when their instances are cheap to verify.
A once-justified generic method can replace repeated discovery/large derivations;
its short instantiation is an O-lane contribution, not necessarily a new SNARK.
No zkVM proof of the agent's search, prompt or elaboration is required—our current
guest already avoids those activities.

Reflection is not “execute untrusted native code and trust its boolean.” Its
correctness theorem and exact quotation/denotation relation must be grounded,
and the computation/certificate checker needed for each application must run in
the trusted logical/checking route or be separately certified. General methods
do not make hard side conditions or large input semantics free. Efficient proof
objects need not exist for every target; there is no general short-proof theorem.

Alternative proofs may change rank/premises and therefore exact J even when the
mathematical statement is equivalent. The semantic ModuleID may remain unchanged
when its committed descriptor and semantic dependencies do; warrant identity
need not. Keep real object/policy identities or
explicitly checked transport; do not bypass exact-J rules to pretend a different
proof is the old artifact. This is a candidate-selection issue, not a license to
rewrite frozen module identities.

### Batching and selected certified frontiers

Already supported A aggregation amortizes repeated setup and packages exact
intermediate journals without requiring an I receipt per intermediate module.
It is not free theorem checking: all newly claimed local extensions and every
actual boundary assumption remain established. Certifying the entire library
is neither necessary nor desirable. Certify an available, useful cut and retain
only the bounded new suffix and demanded transparent semantics at the receiver.

The existing SELECTIVE-CERTIFICATION-POLICY derives conditional batch choice,
cost/capacity bounds and a cut theorem. Reuse them. In particular a seal must
finish before incoming work exhausts suffix capacity, or the protocol must
apply backpressure/refusal. Waiting for “enough popularity” at a full suffix
cannot guarantee continued growth. Batching changes latency, access to member
openings and retention work; no measured affine batch-cost law is currently
available. Blanket batching is not justified by U's single-target cost.

### Composite receipts, delayed compression and hierarchical aggregation

One can in principle expose segment/composite proofs before compressing them,
trading producer work for verifier work and larger evidence. Current adopted
receivers explicitly accept genuine succinct Poseidon2 only; this is a new
profile, not a flag to bypass the existing check.

For U, the 36 saved segment checkpoint envelopes total 8,422,436 bytes. This is
**not** an encoded composite application-receipt size; parent assumptions and
serialization differ. It illustrates the bytes that succinct composition removes.
The recursion-removal ceiling above shows this route alone cannot deliver an
orders-of-magnitude producer saving. A composite still needs complete application
verification and genuine premises; nested unresolved composites can make receiver
work grow with proof history. A deliberately bounded intermediate proof DAG may
be useful for a production service, but cannot replace the final no-history profile
without a separate complexity and soundness argument.

Hierarchical/parallel aggregation can reduce wall-critical-path latency or spread
work; it does not erase total proving cost. More machines may increase supply,
not make an uneconomic per-result policy economical by definition.

### A dedicated judgment witness/circuit: the strongest research candidate

Instead of proving a general RISC-V execution of a parsing/correspondence/kernel
algorithm, define a small, explicit mathematical-judgment witness. Candidate
steps include typed application, universe substitution, authenticated constant
lookup, explicit conversion traces and local declaration extension. Untrusted
software generates the witness; the relation checks the witness rather than
rediscovering evaluation choices or reinterpreting several representations.

Its soundness target must be explicit. Either prove inclusion into the currently
adopted conditional predicate,

\[
\operatorname{CheckTrace}_P(t,w)=(J,\Pi)
\Longrightarrow R_I(\operatorname{encode}(t,w))=(J,\Pi),
\]

with original parser/resource constraints where required, or define a new
Lean-sound judgment profile with its own honest identifiers and correspondence.
The latter is not allowed to masquerade as the old executable's exact predicate.
Both routes retain F/P, exact statement binding, universe/binder scope, semantic
dependencies, every imported premise and recursive discharge. A commitment to
a type-inference result is not evidence that the result is correct.

This could reduce both dynamic checking work and general-machine arithmetization
overhead. However the existing RC experiment already made a narrower language
and still cost 30 segments. A genuinely different proposal must identify which
large repeated work is removed, how the witness certifies it, and its circuit/
trace costs before another build. “Use a specialized checker” alone is not new
evidence. Source-derived operation bounds plus an independently challenged
correspondence argument should precede one narrow execution screen; no proof
campaign is warranted merely by an attractive new acronym.

### Folding/IVC, direct SNARKs, sum-check/GKR and non-ZK arguments

| Route | Potential improvement | Cost/trust issue that remains |
|---|---|---|
| Folding/IVC, such as Nova | Incremental accumulation and different per-step recursion costs; final compression can be deferred | The step relation must still be arithmetized and computed. A folded instance is not automatically a small independently checkable proof; terminal verification/compression and witness handling matter. Branching module DAGs require all parent premises, not an unproved linear-chain analogy. |
| Dedicated SNARK/STARK over judgment witnesses | Avoids universal CPU instruction/memory simulation; can exploit repeated algebraic structure | Circuit generation, hashing/memory/lookup constraints, witness generation, setup/commitment assumptions and checker refinement become central implementation obligations. No measured improvement here. |
| Sum-check/GKR-family interactive proof | Very efficient proving for suitable arithmetic circuits; some constructions have linear-time provers | Arithmetic circuit size/depth/regularity and input commitments matter. Pointer-heavy dependent-type checking is not automatically such a cheap circuit. Sequential interaction and public-transferability requirements need explicit treatment. |
| Non-ZK verifiable computation | Avoids unnecessary witness privacy where all proof data is public | Soundness, commitments and consistency checks remain. No source evidence identifies hiding as the dominant current cost. |
| Interactive checking with an online prover | May trade stored certificate size and preprocessing for online service | An offline transferable warrant is not obtained merely by storing a transcript of convenient challenges. Fresh randomness, commitment order, communication, online availability and any Fiat–Shamir/random-oracle conversion must be specified. |

Replacing RISC Zero with another universal zkVM may improve constants; no available
measurement justifies promising that it removes this bottleneck. Dropping secrecy
is a sensible requirement simplification, not a demonstrated speedup. The first
research artifact should compare relations and full prover/verifier costs, not
start several heavyweight backend builds on this PC.

Trusted signatures/committees/TEEs and optimistic challenge periods can lower
honest-case checking costs only by changing assumptions. They are not equivalent
substitutes for independent immediate mathematical acceptance under the existing
goal. Randomly sampling raw proof steps is not a sound shortcut; a genuine
PCP/IOP-style encoding, commitments and soundness theorem would be additional
machinery, not evidence from ordinary spot checks.

## Ranked changes and decision gates

1. **Make the existing O/C evidence portfolio the explicit product contract.**
   Ordinary valid contribution/publication should not synchronously require a
   new succinct proof. A consumer advertises finite evidence/semantic budgets;
   selection uses grounded caches, native suffixes and already available cuts,
   with a clear strict-profile certificate requirement. Certificates may be
   requested and supplied separately by replaceable producers. Expected payoff
   is high because it avoids unnecessary production entirely and mostly reuses
   installed mechanisms. Failure mode: uncertified first uses have no promised
   cheap strict acceptance; declining or waiting is necessary, not a downgrade
   hidden as success. Confirm only the missing public workflow; do not rebuild
   current parsers, caching, source preparation or frontier selection.
2. **Invest certificates in reusable methods/cuts; use ordinary short witnesses
   for their many applications.** Reuse current A batching and exact-J replacement,
   with bounded tails and explicitly funded capacity renewal. The new decision is
   which supported mathematical workload and cut remove substantial *otherwise
   missing* work—not another generic economics component. The positive O-versus-L
   sharing case remains valid. A C-versus-O claim needs its own matched costs.
   Expected payoff is moderate/high when methods have genuinely diverse reuse;
   zero or negative when caches/alternative short proofs already erase the margin,
   demand is absent, or required semantics dominate. No global research agenda or
   human-intention field is introduced.
3. **Research a proof-object/explicit-judgment relation before a backend swap.**
   Obtain an independently challenged exact semantic/refinement contract and
   source-derived operation/circuit model; compare general-zkVM, direct circuit
   and folding/sum-check choices on that same relation. This is the highest-upside
   producer research but also highest engineering/assurance risk. Current RC
   failed its investment screen; no replay, renamed pass or guessed 10× claim.
   A narrow later experiment is justified only by a decision-changing predicted
   cost reduction and a complete finite envelope. Preserve the current backend
   as a known expensive capability/fallback while this is unresolved.

Do not prioritize another RAM investigation, wrapper speedup as a cure for proof
cost, blanket Groth16 compression, more tiny certificates, or global consensus.
Consumer-operation validation overhead is real and separately worth reducing;
it is not the source of U's 25,216.95 segment-proof CPU seconds.
