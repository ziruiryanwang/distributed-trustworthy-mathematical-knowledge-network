## Decision and marginal payoff

**Recommend one bounded public `produce-baseline` export of the already installed
`Std.HashMap.isEmpty_insert` from `Std.Data.HashMap.Lemmas`, followed by read-only
inspection of its exact closure. No admission, authoring, search or service run
follows automatically.** Root must select the exact inputs and finite allocation.

This tests an engineering unknown: can the current selected producer handle a
real nonbasis library method within its existing local envelope, and what would
independent adoption actually need? Existing limits alone do not determine its
closure, indivisible group sizes, serialization cost or first incompatibility.
Our target product explicitly offers selected inert library interchange; a human
participant is not necessary to qualify that advertised boundary. My initial
abstention conflated missing growth-value evidence with missing coverage evidence.

The expected payoff is modest but concrete: discriminate a practically usable
library transfer route from an unsupported or costly one before more architecture.
It requires no invented theorem, new project, corpus download or compiler build.
The alternative is leaving the central route evidenced mainly by small controlled
exports. A fresh abstraction exercise or another consolidation audit answers less.

## Fixed discriminator and consequences

Use the existing supported runtime/package and that one exact declaration/module.
Pin public producer inputs and a fresh output before one launch; retain its first
failure, complete costs and closure evidence. Choose an existing affordable local
producer envelope, not an allowance enlarged until this library passes. No native
build, new owner, protected-state mutation, dependency installation or network.

On success, inspect the exact target, actual full-unit dependency closure, complete
group membership, total/per-unit bytes and first-route fit. Compare them against
existing admission, receive and cold/anchored realization limits separately; the
8192-unit producer limit is not a receiving or authoring promise. Do not infer fit
from aggregate counts alone. Exported bytes grant no mathematical acceptance.

If it fits a useful existing route, the immediate result is representative producer
coverage plus a concrete candidate for a separately selected adoption/use check.
If it exceeds a receiving/materialization boundary, identify that exact mismatch
before deciding whether ordinary approved-package use already serves the case.
If production refuses, preserve the first cause: source incompatibility, closure
growth and producer whole-environment cost lead to different decisions. A cap
failure is not proof of an inherent impossibility or authority to raise the cap.
