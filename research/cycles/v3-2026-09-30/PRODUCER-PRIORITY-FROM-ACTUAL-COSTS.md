## Consequence for the next producer choice

Holding the actual measured stage work fixed, completely eliminating ALL
other cost from that KNOWN subtotal would remove only 2,953.90625 CPU seconds,
about 6.9047%. Removing a storage-census fraction cannot deliver a 10% saving
of this particular known subtotal on that premise. This is an observed-cost
decomposition, not a bound on arbitrary future runs: unmetered costs, timing
tails, changed workloads, scheduling and changed stage work remain unknown.
Segment stages alone are about 72.2764% of that known subtotal.

Therefore do not prioritize scanner/controller micro-optimization as the main
solution to costly cryptographic production. Its growing-state reliability
and bounded-resource behavior can merit a separate cheap controller-only
test, but that is not a dominant proof-speedup experiment. The central cost
candidate remains reducing authenticated guest trace work before proving,
starting with the already identified Value-to-typed-row parser seam. Its first
selection would be execute-only differential/trace evidence under a new image,
not another full certificate. No such selection occurs in this note.

A changed-image gain must pay for build/adoption, compatibility and any
same-image parent recertification or separately justified bridge. Fewer trace
rows/segments would justify further investigation, not automatically prove
full producer CPU speedup or economically beneficial useful growth.

## What evidence still decides growth

Current production and tiny inner verification times are unmatched scopes;
their ratio is not a speedup or break-even count. Retain the existing full-cost
model in GROWTH-FEASIBILITY and the causal comparison in V3-FINAL-CORE-GAPS.
For a prospective equal-endpoint/equal-opportunity trial, measure each arm's
complete attributable cost once, including search/acquisition/import, failures,
new certificate work and maintenance. A reuse arm may choose a direct proof;
the control cannot be artificially forced to reconstruct a long ancestry.

No new throughput, novelty, mathematical acceptance or net growth claim is
made. This completed-cost observation changes prioritization: large producer
efficiency gains need to address proved stage work, while current source work
addresses cheap useful-candidate discovery. Broad twin goals remain active.
