# Imported exposure and source-turnover triage

Date: 2026-09-20. Root-authored concept decision record; held for independent review. No implementation, native execution, benchmark, new network contract or selected local policy. This small record is both the commissioning trail and the argument; a separate module is not warranted merely to repeat it.

## Decision under examination

Keep an independent agent's explicit choice of imported results to serve as the v1 path. Defer a framework-owned automatic imported-result selector. This is a scope decision, not a claim that automation has no value. Investigate the distinct broker-local source-turnover seam only if it addresses a concrete discovery failure without assuming free supply, source-health knowledge or successful adoption.

Predecessors: the [matched reciprocal path](../modules/broker-economics/matched-reuse-comparison.md) and [repeated-capacity case](../modules/broker-economics/repeated-reuse-capacity.md) establish conditional finite paths, not measured benefit or autonomous source selection. Their exact reviewed cuts and limitations remain unchanged. The latter's direct-only exclusion is not a fully costed failed baseline.

## Execution is not nomination

The selected chain already permits an independent owner/agent to perform the following separately authorized actions:

1. Choose an exact imported replay whose complete support is independently checked and currently protected.
2. Fund current custody and authorize disclosure of the complete selected closure, not merely the root statement.
3. Admit native public service and actual source-local indexing.
4. Separately fund a discovery path, such as the actual source's own same-replay submission to a known broker.
5. Pay actual reader acquisition/checking and the source's continuing obligations; publication does not allocate those resources automatically.

The supporting contracts impose no requirement that the serving owner originally produced the result. [E admission](../../notes/analyses/CP-001E-custody-contract.md) permits owner-selected scopes and reuse of currently checked/protected local evidence. [G targets](../../notes/analyses/CP-001G-structural-retrieval.md) are exact checked, public, retained targets. [Public read scopes](../../spec/OPERATIONAL-CORPUS-V1-NETWORK.md) bind the explicitly authorized collection and checked closure. [F coverage](../../notes/analyses/CP-001F-exact-routing-coverage.md) operates on the owner-selected public cut. [I submission](../../notes/analyses/CP-001I-direct-broker-bindings.md) binds the actual source identity to its own lead. These are distinct guards, not one inferred permission.

The selected local policies have narrower nomination rules:

| Existing policy | What it may decide | What it does not confer |
|---|---|---|
| [K cache selection](../modules/cache-selection/proposal.md) | Discretionary retention across enrolled exact checked results, including imports, using settled local checked-use observations | New acquisition/checking, public disclosure, listing or native upload authority |
| [M producer exposure](../modules/egress-selection/proposal.md) | Paid proof-root exposure to known native contacts after exact settled local producer-output completion | Imported eligibility merely from receiving acceptance, broker submission, HINTs or routed publication |
| Existing owner/agent controls | An explicit funded choice of imported checked evidence to expose using the above execution chain | Autonomous nomination, new supply or guaranteed discoverability |

An imported result cannot be relabeled a local producer-output completion to pass M. Completion/use observations create no callback grant. If an imported-eligibility adapter is later warranted, it must identify its exact settled receiving/checking event and standing caller authority; preserve current assessments, complete inventory/protection, full-closure disclosure, one-shot claims, custody-before-publication, deadlines and explicit drain; and share actual quotas and lifecycle slots with producer candidates. Origin labels cannot multiply grants. Such an adapter would still not add broker or routed discovery to M.

## What a useful replication signal would mean

Validity is unchanged by usefulness ranking. Selection may be heuristic, but its inputs need honest meanings:

| Observation | Supports | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Settled local checked use | This owner has actually reused the exact compatible result | Independent remote demand, novelty or a future return on publication |
| A remote request at this source | An access attempt occurred under its actual observation scope | Completed acquisition, independent demand or checked mathematical benefit; retries and probes differ |
| A committed listing plus current source authority and remaining resources | An observed discovery/service opportunity over its actual interval | Future listing, source admission, timely complete checking or usefulness |
| No observed demand | No recorded demand in the observed exposure window | Mathematical uselessness or lack of demand through other contacts/times |

Genuine repeated inexpensive applications can inflate local use without fraud. A private importer has no own-source remote request history before exposure; requiring demonstrated demand before every exposure is circular. An optional policy would need bounded exploration or an explicit UNKNOWN state. Popularity can make honest agents replicate already well-served results or compete for the same listing slots. Distinct authenticated identities prove neither independent demand nor independent failure domains.

For a decision between continuing private retention and adding public exposure of evidence already received and checked, prior acquisition/checking is sunk **only insofar as both alternatives retain that same starting cut**. Additional custody, guard refresh, extraction/indexing, listing, uploads, maintenance, failed attempts and settlement are marginal costs. If the new policy itself caused the import, those upstream costs are not sunk in the broader comparison. Low use or nonrenewal does not authorize deleting another owner's protection.

## A reachability obstruction, not a mathematical defect

Fix an exact descriptor d and replay R. Let a reader's admitted discovery graph before its deadline contain only one known broker E for finding sources of d. The reader has no separately funded native route or other contact reaching a fifth source X. Suppose:

- E's selected current row contains four distinct source leads, none naming X.
- X has complete currently checked/protected evidence, authorized native service and spare actual resources.
- X's same-replay submission is not selected because the four-lead bound remains occupied; no broker-owner replacement or incumbent withdrawal occurs before the deadline.
- There is no other allowed path by which this reader learns an actionable X contact before that deadline.

Then X supplies **zero additional timely reachable capacity to this reader through this graph**, regardless of local-use score or spare upload. The conclusion follows by enumerating the permitted discovery edges: every broker page contains only selected leads from its frozen cut, and no admitted edge reveals X. It is not a bound on X's physical throughput or a global network impossibility. If some incumbent is adequate, the reader may succeed through it; the zero concerns the added X path.

A stronger honest failure case makes all four incumbent sources unavailable after ungraceful stops while E retains their metadata. With no alternative path, the listed candidates cannot supply the required evidence before the deadline even though X could do so if reached. Safety remains intact: no failed candidate becomes an accepted theorem. Liveness of this offered service fails.

A prior query observing a vacant slot does not reserve that slot. Operation 21's actual serialized decision may see it filled. Nor does DECLINED or DEFERRED_NO_PROMISE promise a future queue position. Paid preparation and failed listing remain charged; the zero-reachability argument is not a numerical costed failed-execution trace.

## Query expiry does not retire source leads

I gives a finite issuer-local lifetime to **query snapshots** and permits END retirement. This retires query authority/allocations under their separate release conditions, not the current descriptor row's leads. The lead grammar contains no lease/expiry field and the selected contract supplies no automatic native-health retirement. It explicitly says that source-native availability changes do not automatically change broker state.

Consequently, waiting for a query snapshot to expire and starting a fresh query need not change the source set at all. The selected rules impose no finite upper bound on the stale-full-row obstruction. For any finite horizon, unchanged row selection is an allowed case; that does not assert unlimited funded queries or infinite source obligations from a finite grant.

The [local retry policy](../../notes/analyses/CP-001I-broker-local-retry-policy.md) rotates among the **received** at-most-four leads under actual continuing admission, persistent eligibility and retained cursor premises. It cannot reveal X or mutate E. Its at-most-one-hour transient eligibility bound is neither source recovery nor a source-list replacement deadline. A native failure observation is not a theorem-falsity judgment or permission to evict a foreign lead.

Existing ways out remain real, but conditional:

| Existing event | What changes | What remains necessary |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent recovery | A currently discoverable source may become usable | Actual authority, complete support, reader/source grants and sufficient time |
| Source self-withdrawal | Removes its current self lead at a serialized cut | Reachable authorized caller and paid mutation; unresolved restoring intents/ABA remain governed by I |
| Broker-owner selection change | Owner may separately select a different bounded lead set | Actual authorized local decision, finite resources and current-cut commit; op21 cannot itself evict someone else |
| Another already usable broker/native path | May expose X or another adequate source | Actual contact, admitted work, listing/routing coverage and sufficient time; not global rendezvous |
| Fresh query after a relevant change | May observe the changed selected set | Fresh funded query/current snapshot, adequate scan and subsequent source service |

Thus the obstruction exposes an optional **selection/turnover policy seam**, not a missing wire operation or a contradiction in I's deliberately conditional discovery guarantee. Earlier [I economics review](../../notes/audits/CP-001I-broker-economics-cross-audit.md) already required an actual path when counting more sources than one row can expose. The present refinement is specifically that query turnover and reader retries supply no source-turnover guarantee.

## An information limit on automatic turnover

Consider two worlds with one initially selected source lead l and the same broker initial state. In H the source remains healthy for the stipulated later reader; in D it has crashed. Up to an inclusive broker decision cut C, give the broker identical admitted external inputs, scheduling/clock observations and owner decisions, with no observation distinguishing source health. Couple the same random-coin distribution so that these inputs agree for every relevant paired coin realization, not merely one coincident transcript. The broker's local transition rule then gives identical selection traces by induction on its transitions. This assumes identical input histories, not identical internal states as a conclusion smuggled into the premise.

Let KEEP mean l remains selected continuously through **and including** C; let REMOVE mean l is absent at C. Under the coupled trace these events are disjoint. Consequently, for the same randomized policy,

\[
\Pr_H[\mathrm{KEEP}] + \Pr_D[\mathrm{REMOVE}] \le 1.
\]

In particular no such policy guarantees both uninterrupted healthy listing through C and removal of the crashed listing by that same inclusive cut. Requiring healthy retention only strictly before C would not give this conclusion: removing at C could satisfy both weaker conditions. Removing and reinstalling before C satisfies neither KEEP nor REMOVE, which is why the displayed bound is an inequality rather than an asserted equality.

This is a conditional observational limit, not a general impossibility of useful turnover, fault detection or decentralized availability. An actual distinguishing observation breaks the premise; probing itself needs authority/resources and establishes only its scoped observation, not future uninterrupted service. A practical heuristic may deliberately trade the two risks, using budgeted exploration and empirical evaluation. No perfect health oracle is required for the project.

The smallest regression for blind finite tenure is concrete: a healthy sole source lists once, has no later listing work scheduled, and is removed at tenure end. An E-only reader arriving afterward loses the discovery path that indefinite retention would preserve. Conversely, a fifth source declined before tenure end gains no visibility merely because a slot later opens: it has no queued promise and may never make another funded submission. Neither case concerns mathematical validity.

## Consequential next decision and stop rule

Do not open a broad replication or reputation module. The next bounded candidate is an optional broker-local rule that gives finite exposure tenure to selected source leads, using existing owner-local selection rather than adding an eviction right to self-submit. A nonrenewable tenure is only a candidate to investigate, **not a specified or selected policy**. No TTL is retroactively read into I; no source freshness attestation, successful adoption or verified-health oracle is supplied.

The investigation is worth continuing only if it can state an actual conditional escape from the stale-full-row case and its regression. It must address:

- Admission versus renewal/no-op semantics without changing ALREADY_LISTED by implication.
- Original selection-time identity, qualified clock/restart handling, serialized removal and bounded old-snapshot/result retention. A delayed removal must bind the installed incarnation, not only LeadDigest: I's permitted removal/reinsertion ABA can recreate the same lead bytes. Endpoint updates and reinstalls need explicit age treatment.
- Separately funded local selection work, cleanup, source resubmission and reader refresh; no hidden timer or grant.
- Competition: a vacant slot can be lost before X retries. Finite stale-row retirement alone is not fair source admission.
- Regression: healthy incumbents may disappear, sources may miss re-entry and readers may retain historical pages. More churn can reduce useful service.
- Complete paid unsuccessful attempts and exposure loss in any comparison; no popularity-optimality proof is required.

In particular, maintaining N continuously exposed entries at b brokers with finite tenure tau would require repeated successful admission/removal work at a rate on the order of bN/tau in a long-running steady deployment, before retries and retained versions. This is an indicative accounting warning under continuing-coverage premises, not a selected rate, actual-cost lower bound for every policy, or supplied future grant. Synchronized expiry can create a burst and a coverage gap even when such an average rate fits.

If the benefit needs a new reservation queue, health-probing service, global clock, new broker rendezvous or unbounded candidate pool, state that expansion and reassess before adding it. If existing explicit owner intervention is sufficient for the chosen service, leave the optional automatic rule unselected. The objective is a reliable autonomous commons under stated supply and behavior conditions, not universal liveness for every finite contact graph.

All native and reciprocal execution gates remain NOT RUN. This investigation neither reopens the completed capacity-source arithmetic nor discharges complete Lean bootstrap/export/check/use, empirical usefulness, continuing supply or large-network qualification.
