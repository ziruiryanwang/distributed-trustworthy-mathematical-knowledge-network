### Why this correction is justified without a larger benchmark

For an offer round with m providers, residual asynchronous time R and closure
reserve c = 1 second, each attempted exchange gets at most min(5 seconds,
floor(R/m) - c), with implementation units/rounding in milliseconds. In the
idealized model where closure takes at most c and local work is negligible,
an attempt consumes at most R/m: after removing that provider, the next share
is at least the old R/m. Positive initial slices therefore give every provider
an opportunity in that offer round, subject also to sufficient aggregate tickets
and bytes. This is a conditional allocation argument, not measured latency.

The implementation deliberately makes weaker unconditional claims: local CPU,
OS scheduling, uncertain connection cleanup and exhausted request/scan/byte grants
can stop the round. It preserves those charges/failures instead of renewing a
budget or falsely claiming coverage. Page rounds prevent an early provider from
draining every page before another active provider gets a turn. Work still scales
with selected contacts and bounded examined candidates, not a mandatory global
corpus scan; useful relevance and routing coverage remain separate obligations.
