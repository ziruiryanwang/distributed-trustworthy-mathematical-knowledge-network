# Delayed retrieval after independent agent deliberation

2026-09-23, America/Chicago. Concept and next-test checkpoint. This note changes no U1 arm, product code, fixture, ledger, or outcome. It does not reinterpret a failed mathematical attempt as a successful one.

## Observation and precise question

Arm A's first `peer_find` returned an authenticated seven-entry, candidate-only control catalog. Its sole service then stopped naturally at `2026-09-23T05:06:12.944633Z`, with post-close elapsed 600.0152165 seconds, only two connections and 19,511 charged send bytes. The agent's one-shot `peer_acquire` began 44.644902 seconds *after* the service stopped and returned `REFUSED/TRANSFER_TIMEOUT` with no fetched sequence or admission. The [independent receipt audit](../../experiments/useful-reuse-u1/U1-ARM-A-SERVICE-AND-ACQUIRE-INDEPENDENT-AUDIT.md) binds those times and files. This is an availability failure of a selected candidate, not a false theorem, malicious-peer finding, or evidence that the control catalog contained the useful treatment theorem. A qualified service terminal and a qualified refusal receipt did not produce useful acquisition.

The needed property is **retrieval after an unbounded decision interval, conditional on surviving reachable custody**. Let `h` be the SHA-256 of one exact framed bundle, `r` its canonical mathematical root, and `F` its foundation. `Seen_R(h,r,F)` means receiver R has a catalog claim, still only a hint. `Have_H(h)` means holder H has exact verified bytes. `Accept_R(F,Γ,h)` is R's separate fresh local checker judgment in context `Γ`. Safety requires that `Seen`, holder identity, a transport hash, and custody never imply `Accept`; all received closure, metadata and proof obligations still pass R's selected Lean checking policy. Assuming SHA-256 collision resistance and stable locally pinned files, matching `h` across holders identifies the exact transported representation. It need not identify all mathematically equivalent repackagings.

No finite window beginning at the first `peer_find` guarantees later acquisition when an independent agent's deliberation time is unbounded. A bounded liveness claim must quantify its premises: at least one holder in R's reachable peer set retains `h`, exposes it during some interval long enough for discovery and bounded transfer, R eventually probes that holder during that interval, quotas/storage/network remain available, and the operator or agent has not stopped. Under those premises, a finite search over known peers and local digest comparison can eventually deliver `Have_R(h)`. If every copy is lost, every holder remains offline, or R never probes a reachable holder, no decentralized protocol can promise retrieval. Authentication of a configured endpoint may be needed for routing; political attribution is not.

## Candidate mechanisms and decision

| Mechanism | What it helps | What it cannot guarantee; principal cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Longer single TTL or repeated finite sessions | More time for one provider's catalog and bundles; existing finite hosting can continue by explicit owner grants. | Any finite fixed TTL can again be exceeded by deliberation or operator delay. Repeated sessions require the same owner to remain willing, online, reachable and funded; wall occupancy, port/process capacity and grants grow with coverage even if CPU is low. A's 600-second service used only 0.484375 enclosing Job CPU seconds yet still expired before acquisition. |
| Eager acquisition immediately after catalog selection | Turns a short live service window into durable local bytes before deep proof work. | It is an agent policy, not an availability guarantee. Prefetching `k` candidates spends roughly the sum of their bundle bytes, validation, storage, request and stage budgets; unused or junk candidates make this expensive. The agent still must select candidates without a human hint. |
| Durable custody at multiple holders | Exact bytes can outlive the producer and be served by a surviving holder. The existing [replica-native-02 test](../../software/qualification/replica-native-02/OUTCOME.md) already demonstrates producer-offline exact-byte re-serving and fresh receiver use on one host. | Replication alone does not tell R where a live copy is. Each additional copy costs roughly one bundle's durable storage, transfer, revalidation and serving capacity. Custody may later disappear unless holders maintain it. |
| Content-addressed lookup over configured peers | R remembers `h`, searches other known catalogs after the original endpoint expires, checks an alternate row's same `h`, then acquires exact bytes from that holder. Catalogs/holder hints are untrusted; local admission remains authoritative. | A local list of aliases is not a global directory. Search costs scale with peers/catalog pages and may return stale hints. Current peer handles are endpoint-bound, so R must ordinarily `peer_find` the alternative holder and select its returned handle; a future local digest-to-holder cache could automate this without consensus or a mandatory central server. |

**Smallest next test:** use the already implemented `replica_expose`, two configured peer aliases and existing exact bundle digests. Do not change the frozen U1 arms. In a fresh three-node allocation, P offers one genuine checked result with digest `h`; H ordinarily acquires it and explicitly commits an exact-byte replica view. R independently discovers the result from P's returned catalog and records its chosen `h` without operator selection. Stop P's finite service. Prospectively impose a deliberation gap longer than P's remaining service lifetime, then give H one later, separately budgeted finite service slot. R must independently search its configured peers, find H's row with the same `h`, acquire through H's newly returned remote handle, freshly admit the closure under `F`, and substantively use it in a distinct checked publication if claiming mathematical continuation. Freeze the result, delay, agent packet, peer order, grants, stage/request/CPU/storage limits and stop rules before R starts. Record a same-delay one-holder comparison or use A's preserved expiration only as a qualitative failure precedent, never as a matched numerical control.

This test has a clear negative: even though H possesses and serves the exact bytes during the declared interval, R cannot find/acquire/admit them within the frozen budget, or an alternate holder's metadata/bytes mismatch is mistakenly accepted. A pass would establish one bounded delayed-retrieval and continuation instance, not global availability or a general probability of success. Measure discover-to-acquire delay, holder uptime, probes/pages, catalog/bundle bytes, rechecks, retained bytes per holder, native stages, disjoint Job CPU, agent/model cost where observable, failed attempts and service wall occupancy. If ordinary two-peer lookup proves cumbersome, the smallest subsequent product change is a bounded local `h -> known configured holders` hint/cache and a retrying resolver that rechecks every selected catalog and bundle. Its hints must never become mathematical acceptance or a requirement for a global ledger.
