# Source-derived proof reconciliation: result and next central decision

Date: 2026-09-20. Executed and independently audited R1-PASS at the held bounded scope. Whole-project objective remains unfinished.

## Executed boundary

The bounded [R1 experiment](../../experiments/lean-proof-context-reconciliation/RESULTS.md) has completed one actual source-derived reconciliation case. Its second isolated producer supplied the same map definition and helper statement with a different exact helper proof. Both original contexts checked individually; unchanged ordinary merge rejected their conflicting choices at the intended boundary.

The existing PI1 route then grounded a finite observed prerequisite graph, constructed exact newly bound replays, cold-reconstructed a coherent environment, and elaborated an ordinary-source consumer. Crucially, the consumer's stored type retains the shared map and hence its selected helper `p`, while its proof directly applies the other exact helper `q`. The exact closure retains both proofs. Actual native inverse export, fresh replay and second inverse export produced identical packages. Independent native/source inspection verifies the seven-binder proof application and unchanged shared identifiers; the independent receipt/graph reviewer authenticated the actual records, recomputed the grounding and qualified the complete bounded result.

See the [audit ledger](../../experiments/lean-proof-context-reconciliation/AUDIT.md) for independence, inherited trusted boundaries and exact review coverage. The frozen contract remains historical pre-execution evidence; no earlier NOT RUN record is rewritten to pretend that implementation was already complete.

## What this resolves

Honest agents need not choose the same exact proof merely to contribute the same mathematical dependency. For this observed case, the receiver can preserve both immutable proofs while selecting one local realization of a shared semantic alias. The receiver checks the newly assembled environment and actual application; it neither overwrites an old alias in place nor reuses a certificate from a different proof context.

The finite graph's topological grounding supplies a candidate reconstruction order, not a typing theorem. Native conversion is a separate obligation. Both alternatives became ready in the same actual round; the specified typed-ID tie break selected `p`. This establishes neither least-cost selection nor global preferred evidence. Proof-sensitive computations beyond this favorable data-projection case remain subject to REDUCE1's existing limitations.

No global proof-election ledger, shared mathematical goal or attribution authority was needed for this test. This is a concrete reason not to make blockchain consensus a prerequisite of the mathematical exchange core. Availability, discovery, resource allocation and hostile-input containment remain distinct obligations.

## Next decision: full formal discovery/application, without a forced usefulness claim

Return now to B-UTILITY. Do not extend R1 into more proof-substitution variants absent a new demonstrated core blocker.

Two independent next-step assessments agree that the present Subtype case is too easy to demonstrate a meaningful general efficiency advantage: direct construction and standard `Subtype.ext` must stay available. Likewise a proposed finite-path certificate workload cannot be compared only against bounded generic proof search while withholding ordinary BFS/transitive-closure computation. Such comparisons would measure a chosen solver weakness, not convincingly demonstrate useful accumulation.

The next bounded question is therefore the **full original Gate B discovery/application capability**, with usefulness measured separately and no expected win. Do not replace its existential target by projection-injectivity alone: that would drop discovery of definitions, non-propositional witness construction, subsequent queries involving newly acquired interfaces, and joint application. A narrowed case, if needed, is at most a single labelled smoke test of the same controller, not another research destination.

Reuse existing Lean `SolveByElim` and native application/backtracking APIs as an optional local controller. A small orchestration layer must expose real data-witness obligations, derive formal queries, acquire/check candidates, admit only actual checked declarations and restart cleanly after environment extension. Include definitions as candidates. Keep standard/local proving available in every arm. Any generic projection-congruence support must be precisely bounded and common to every arm.

Resident-library search is not decentralized discovery: its suggested names already refer to declarations in the local environment. External descriptors supply candidate leads, not proof authority. The missing concrete work is the faithful STRUCT2/local snapshot adapter and controller coupling, followed by an exposed-input freeze and actual checked successor/replay. No model API, general new prover or network deployment is selected by this decision.

The three existing access arms remain local-only, oracle-assisted known-ID, and unknown-ID discovery. A direct local solution is legitimate in all three; acquisition cannot be forced merely to improve the report. A predetermined query-first exposure schedule may be tested, but its overhead and any lack of benefit must be reported. The evaluator's source labels, useful IDs, Gate A proof and prior conversation must not enter the discovery controller's allowed input state.

The user's mathematical-commons requirements stay unchanged: agents choose their own investigations; local formal obligations are transient client work, not a public unsolved-task registry or a human-intention admission rule. Formal correctness remains mandatory; search, ranking and retention may remain explicitly heuristic.
