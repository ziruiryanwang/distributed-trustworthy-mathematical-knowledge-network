## Initial findings requiring coordinated repair

### BDR-001 — Arrival publication commits are not a partition of actual builds

The initial source defines `A_p` as successful nonempty arrival publication commits, `I_p` as other successful catalog rebuilds, `F_p` as failed/abandoned build attempts and `Z_p` as admitted initial preparations. It then calls

`B = sum_p (Z_p + A_p + I_p + F_p)`

the exact number of actual build attempts. The definitions do not establish that identity.

Concrete allowed ambiguity: one nonempty arrival commit occurs, its only catalog attempt fails, and no successful build follows during the finite horizon. Then `A_p=1` and `F_p=1`, while only one actual attempt occurred. Alternatively two commits can occur before one paid build, or an admitted initial preparation might not run. Declaring that there are no other attempts does not establish a bijection or make an admitted job an actual event.

The subsequent O(NK) upper-envelope idea can survive, but the claimed exact accounting and its arrival injection must be explicit. One sufficient repair is a disjoint partition of **actual** attempts into successful initial builds, successful arrival-associated builds, successful other builds and failed/abandoned attempts. Associate each successful arrival build with a distinct nonempty arrival commit, with each such commit consuming a distinct previously uncommitted assigned arrival. Then the actual successful arrival-build count is at most N, and failed initial/arrival attempts belong only to the failed class. Other partition choices are possible if their mapping and allowance versus actual-count status are equally explicit.

This finding does not prove that bounded domains fail; it prevents double-counted or unperformed jobs from being reported as exact executed attempt populations. Root independently confirmed the category/bijection problem.

### BDR-002 — Current locator rows and actual visits are not selected-output cardinalities

Two adjacent count distinctions need clarification together:

1. The held catalog inserts coherent current locator rows that can be nonselected. Bounding selected targets and selected exact descriptor/replay alternatives therefore does not by itself bound all current rows n. Account for all other current rows explicitly, or independently supply `K_n` as the main source already requires. Historical/excluded traversal beyond current rows separately belongs to e.
2. The initial source says a one-feature-descriptor-per-selected-target premise and no repeated physical traversal can bound i by `32k`. But i denotes **actual token/posting visits** during preparation, not the number of distinct logical postings. Enumerating expected tokens and the actual relation for both-inclusion checks can already visit a logical posting more than once without revisiting any physical layout page. Sorting/comparison adds its actual work too.

The valid cardinality fact is at most 32k distinct logical selected descriptor/token postings under the stated one-descriptor premise. It is not an i bound. Actual i and physical p/bytes remain independently supplied complete preparation quantities. The main separate caps and complete-inventory requirements are sound; this repair removes an adjacent shortcut that would undermine them. Root independently identified the nonselected-current-row point and agreed with the visit/cardinality distinction.

### Exact closure-family arithmetic and evidence grade

For the supplied family N=mk, the i-th consecutive k-target domain must materialize the union through x_(ik). With pairwise distinct positive-size units of s charged bytes in the chosen independent representation, its required payload is at least sik. Therefore

`sum_(i=1..m) s*i*k = s*k*m*(m+1)/2 = s*(N*N/(2*k)+N/2)`.

The final provider's lower payload is sN. A payload cap C with sN>C defeats that provider under these exact representation/closure assumptions, regardless of the selected-target cap. At k=1 the aggregate is `s*N*(N+1)/2`; one actually shared union of those materialized units is sN. These are not whole-system cost comparisons: native checking, metadata, current support, staging and incumbent service remain additional.

The source correctly presents a finite **conditional exact-evidence dependency family**, not a generated Lean corpus. Target number alone does not force that closure geometry; alternate permitted proofs, qualified compression or other custody architectures can change it. For maximum clarity, the generic X_p/size(x) lower-bound paragraph should explicitly say that its materialized counted units occupy disjoint charged byte allocations. The existing independently materialized representation premise supports this intended reading; this is a clarification rather than a separate blocking finding.

The contrasting family `m*C0 + N*d` is a separately supplied upper envelope for a fixed core and bounded disjoint increments, not an optimistic rewrite of the chain family. Its O(N) payload conclusion requires fixed k/C0/d and m proportional to N/k, and still does not supply cold checking, RAM, serve work or routing fit. Mathematical locality may be a useful heuristic but does not establish those premises.

### Holder capacity and standing supply

The source's fixed-four-holder countercase is intentionally narrow and correct. A stronger optional but unnecessary check illustrates the same distinction: if exactly four depth-zero copies for P providers must all fit among the same 16 supplied holders, with no other ads, total cell slots require `4P <= 16*4096`. Thus P=16,385 defeats that depth-zero population even with ideal balancing. It still does not defeat a separately funded deeper cell tree. Real bytes, incumbent ads, node-wide totals and service queues may make the actual boundary earlier.

Retaining all old domains while adding new ones requires cumulative supply. A bounded per-domain current input is not a bound on all domains' leases, scans or bytes. The source does not claim that fixed total resources sustain an indefinitely growing incumbent population. Actual finite capacity admission can refuse new service; that refusal must remain a failed contribution opportunity in the fixed offer rather than a positive growth result.
