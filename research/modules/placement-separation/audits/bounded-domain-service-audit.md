## Independent check of the unfamiliar-reader countercase

G's initial chosen interactive attempt permits at most 24 provider FIND pages, 16 route-cell lookups, four concurrent peers, and its separate traffic, memory, local CPU, wall-time and type-inspection ceilings. A concurrency cap is not a total-provider cap. The inherited directory has capacity for 256 contacts, so 25 candidate providers are not excluded by contact cardinality alone. Their actual contacts, negotiation, admission and traffic still need to fit the supplied session/offer; a later hint cannot enlarge a pinned lookup session for free.

The source's supplied case has 25 domains, each with two current descriptors carrying the chosen token: a decoy ad anchor and a nonanchor entry. Every decoy and 24 nonanchor entries fail the common query pattern; the remaining nonanchor entry passes and supports the designated checked application. The chosen G policy learns unknown candidates through FIND. Actual feature/pattern/native realizability and work bounds are explicitly hypothetical, not fabricated as an executed Lean corpus.

The two key counts are:

| Policy | Required scoped inspections under the supplied premise | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Split domains | Up to one complete FIND page per two-entry provider; the useful provider may be outside any chosen 24-provider prefix | A 24-page attempt cannot guarantee finding that provider |
| One coherent provider | 50 descriptor pattern inspections; at most 32 such probes per page | Two pages suffice **only if** the actual offered byte/node-pair/CPU/service bounds impose no earlier stop |

The source explicitly supplies the latter no-earlier-stop condition. It does not infer achieved throughput from a maximum of 32 probes or two wall-seconds per page. An empty partial page can advance across fully inspected mismatches using the inherited exact continuation; only the one passing descriptor need be returned. It is consequently within the 32-type initial client cap, while complete acquisition and checking remain separately admitted. This example is not a general argument that every 50-row provider is cheaper than 25 smaller providers.

All 25 routing ads can, in a separately supplied favorable route cut, fit a single at-most-64-ad ROUTE_LIST page. Even maximum 4096-byte ad payloads account for only 102400 bytes before bounded framing, below the 256-KiB reply ceiling in a fitting actual encoding. Thus the fanout obstruction need not rely on failed routing, infinite discovery or a saturated hot cell. The source assumes the actual route/native/calendar costs otherwise fit; that assumption is not an observed network trace. Every resulting FIND is still a separately admitted query.

For the general indistinguishable-provider family, let the winning provider be uniform independently of the client's adaptive/randomized choice and let nonwinning observations reveal no correlated information. After q distinct probes, at most q of P possible winning locations have been inspected. Success probability is at most `min(1,q/P)`. For deterministic probing with q<P, a placement outside the inspected prefix defeats a universal success claim. This argument concerns the stated information model and chosen candidate-discovery interface; informative metadata, a helpful learned prior, exact-ID knowledge or a different paid acquisition strategy changes the premises.

The decoy and chosen-policy premises matter. The argument is not a cryptographic theorem that arbitrary native evidence or every possible local mathematical computation reveals no useful result. A different policy that imports extra evidence, exploits other keys or directly constructs a proof needs its own cost and service comparison. The source appropriately does not use this case to rule out all decentralized retrieval or to make heuristic ranking a truth authority.
