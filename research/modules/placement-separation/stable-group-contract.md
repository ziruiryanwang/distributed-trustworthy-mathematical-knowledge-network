## Symbolic protocol boundary

`SG1`, `CAP_SG1`, `MODE_STABLE_FEATURE`, `SG_OPEN`, `SG_BATCH`, `SG_RENEW`, `SG_CLOSE`, `SG_RESULT` and `SG_ERROR` are **unallocated symbolic identifiers**, not usable opcodes, capability numbers or registered object types. All array grammars in this note are exact candidate abstract payloads. Numeric encodings for these symbols and their typed result bodies must be allocated together in a future separately selected binding manifest. Nothing in this note authorizes transmitting a symbolic string in an existing integer opcode slot.

That manifest, denoted `Binding`, must fix the candidate revision, symbols, canonical codecs, allowed existing record types, result schemas, limits and the complete consumer table below. `BindingID` commits to its complete canonical bytes under a distinct declared domain. Its actual bytes/digest are unavailable until allocation; no provisional digest is a recognized network profile. A mismatch of complete bindings is unsupported, even when both are informally called SG1.

Existing HELLO_REPLY carries capability codes, **not a BindingID field**. Future installation of the complete selected candidate must establish a locally allowlisted immutable mapping from the dedicated allocated `CAP_SG1` capability code to exactly one BindingID and its installed codec/limits/consumer semantics. That code may not refer to several selectable binding revisions. A distinct binding revision requires a distinct dedicated mapping/capability allocation; a node lacking that installed mapping treats it as unsupported. This note neither allocates that code nor extends the HELLO grammar.

All candidate calls require reciprocal authenticated observation of capabilities 5, 6, 7, 8 and that dedicated capability code through the existing HELLO_REPLY exchange. The locally installed one-code/one-binding mapping determines the required BindingID; every candidate call and result then checks its explicit BindingID against that mapping, the exact invocation and stored group state. A peer advertising the code but sending another BindingID is rejected rather than selecting a different decoder or limit vector. Receiving a remote manifest, hash or friendly profile name cannot install a codec, revise the allowlist, change limits or adopt a mathematical foundation. The existing capability-count/message limits still apply. A routing holder needs the named codecs, not local adoption of the mathematical foundation. Native acceptance by a consumer still requires its own adopted and qualified foundation. Structural FIND additionally retains its separate capability-3 requirement.

Old operation 16 creates only its specified FULL_EXACT mode; old operation 20 creates only its specified immutable-FeatureScope FULL_FEATURE mode. Their argument/result schemas are unchanged. Existing operations 17–19 accept only their existing mode branches. **The candidate uses a separately bound symbolic family even for BATCH/RENEW/CLOSE**, with inherited transaction semantics but unambiguous new request/result dispatch. Passing an AuthorityID as an old ScopeID is a mismatch, not fallback. A peer without the new binding receives no SG call; the producer may independently admit an old-mode or ungrouped path with that path's actual costs, or report UNSUPPORTED. It never silently downgrades an established group's ownership.

## Stable identity, durable ownership and nonreuse

For authenticated provider `p`, exact code-4 FoundationID `F` and supported STRUCT2 ExtractorID `E`, define the immutable local authority tuple

`AuthorityID A = [p,F,E,providerAuthorityGeneration16,namespaceSerial64]`.

The last two fields form an authority-generation/serial pair, not a content hash of a theorem collection. The 16-byte generation belongs to p's durable single-writer operational authority; serial is an unsigned, nonwrapping 64-bit integer. A separately domain-separated canonical digest of A may index local tables but never substitutes for checking the complete tuple. The tuple is inert, public operational identity, not a bearer capability, native judgment or promise of unlimited storage.

Before A can appear in an outbound call, its serial is atomically reserved, durably advances the next-serial high-water mark and receives its finite count/byte/lifecycle allocation. Serial reservations are never returned, including aborted creation. Exhaustion stops new creation. A normal restart retains the authority generation, high-water mark, live authority records, support fences, per-key ownership and invocation/spending history. Retiring A does not reset any counter.

Generation uniqueness is a local trusted-authority precondition, not a distributed uniqueness oracle. Where nonrollback durable history can establish an unused generation, installation records that transition before use. If the old generation history or spend continuity is uncertain, the source fences it and cannot reuse it by guessing a large counter. It requires independently admitted new authority with nonreuse established under the existing recovery boundary; when this cannot be established for p, a fresh authenticated provider authority/identity is required. Random fresh identifiers or public keys provide their ordinary probabilistic collision assumption, not an absolute mathematical proof of never having been used. The initial v1 candidate does not solve malicious cloning of one provider's secret key. Such a clone cannot manufacture native truth; it can disrupt that provider's operational ownership and is outside the honest-single-writer service premise.

The local high-water mark compresses retired serial nonreuse; it does not erase still-charged groups, outcomes, pins, migration records or snapshots. All active, opening, retiring, uncertain and unreclaimed authority generations share provider-wide finite resource ceilings. There is no free allocation per new name or identity.

At holder h, groupID remains F's exact **24 bytes total**: 16-byte holder authority-generation identifier followed by the 8-byte big-endian never-reused uint64 group counter. Checked stateVersion remains uint64 and never wraps. The immutable group binding is

`[BindingID,h,groupID,p,MODE_STABLE_FEATURE,F,E,A]`.

At most one ACTIVE group, **including an empty ACTIVE group**, exists for each exact `(h,p,F,E,A)`. OPEN lookup, opening reservations, expiry, recovery and group creation serialize through the holder's authoritative writer. Concurrent matching OPENs cannot each pass an absence test and create duplicate ACTIVE groups. Old CLOSED/EXPIRED/unreclaimed groups, opening/uncertain reservations and old-mode overlap remain separately charged. Capacity exhaustion in this group is not permission for a second active group under the same A; use a separately admitted authority or decline.

Positive requestedLeaseMillis is at most 172800000, as in F. A must agree exactly with authenticated provider, stored F/E/mode and Binding. No call mutates immutable bindings. `[BindingID,holderNodeID,groupID,A,MODE_STABLE_FEATURE,stateVersion,lifecycleCode,memberRoot,memberCount,memberBytes,leaseObservation]`.

LifecycleCode and leaseObservation retain F's exact ACTIVE/CLOSED/EXPIRED and absent/present observation forms. Binding, A and mode in every observation are checked against both the exact originating call and coherent saved group state. Immutable result metadata binds the exact invocation and typed result body; reply delivery has its own existing paid once-only allocation. A copied result cannot spend, renew or authorize native reliance.

Candidate errors use `SG_ERROR` with `[reasonCode,groupObservationOption]`, where a present observation has the SG_RESULT observation grammar, not F's old scope-shaped grammar. Existing F route reason codes and their top-level RESULT mappings are inherited unchanged. In particular unsupported binding/mode uses UNSUPPORTED_ROUTE_PROFILE; wrong caller/holder uses PROVIDER_OR_HOLDER_MISMATCH; wrong exact root/version uses STATE_VERSION_OR_ROOT_MISMATCH; exhausted counters/limits, row-owner conflict, expired/closed/unknown groups, missing expected row and temporary refusal keep their selected mappings. Existing NET REQUEST_MISMATCH/offer/replay/cursor/execution errors remain ordinary NET errors; no new top-level code or invented route enum value is introduced. Contradictory redundant argument bindings receive the appropriate inherited request/profile/identity failure before mutation. An unauthorized caller receives no private observation.

An old operation addressing an SG group is rejected by the old-mode whitelist before any group transition, even if all common fields happen to parse. Conversely an SG operation addressing an old group is rejected. Results from another binding/mode are not reconciled as this call's outcomes. A codec unable to decode the new symbolic-family allocation after future installation returns the ordinary unsupported-operation/profile failure without guessing the old result shape.

## Holder transitions inherited without semantic weakening

OPEN either creates one counted empty group at version zero with admitted finite lease, or returns the matching existing ACTIVE group's exact observation **without extending its lease**. Matching OPEN is idempotent about group creation, not free control work. If the former group expired, a new independently admitted OPEN has a fresh groupID and empty root. The producer cannot mistake it for reconstructed membership. Group records and actual PUT row capacity are separately admitted.

The membership map remains the exact `0x8023/1` cell-bearing Patricia grammar, key-domain/hash, collision failure and 2048-byte node ceiling in F. Under this explicit new binding its leaves admit only existing ProviderAd2 `0x8022/2` K4 records at exact F/E, anchored to PublicIndexDescriptor3 and InstanceReplay3 as in G. The map is operational metadata, not permission to fetch it remotely or a theorem certificate. No ad gains an AuthorityID field; the group state carries ownership.

Mutations remain `[0,cell,signedProviderAd]` PUT and `[1,cell,expectedAdCID]` DROP. A batch has at most 64 mutations and 64 KiB total control body, sorted by canonical membership key, with at most one mutation per key. The added candidate header counts inside that byte ceiling; it can lower effective batch width. Signature, identity, F/E/key/cell, ad revision, exact expected rows, disclosure, capacity and group root/version/lease are checked. Equal revision/equal bytes is idempotent; equal revision/different bytes or stale revision fails the entire batch. No partial application occurs. BATCH does not extend a lease.

A changed transaction atomically installs all new map/row/count/byte state and advances version once. Count/byte aggregates are writer-maintained exact state, never caller assertions. Staging, reply bytes, control records, actual retained byte-time, old map generations and snapshots require admission. Existing base ungrouped PUT/DROP and old group operations cannot bypass SG ownership. A cell/provider row has one live owner; old-owner DROP must settle before same-cell new-owner PUT.

RENEW checks the exact active group/version/root and pays the actual extension using F's `max(oldDeadline,t+delta)` rule bounded by holder-local `t+48 hours`. It advances version once without changing memberRoot. It cannot resurrect forgotten membership or reset spend. CLOSE gates current serving immediately, advances the applicable version/lifecycle under the inherited rule, and schedules only paid generation-fenced reclamation. An empty group still has a lease/control allocation; closing it does not erase charged history.

Expiry and CLOSE gate all current rows without scanning them for immediate physical deletion. Physical cleanup checks exact old group/storage generations; delayed old cleanup cannot remove a replacement row. Snapshot-held old state and every unresolved outcome remain charged. There is no new GC acknowledgment, private inventory service or global cancellation operation.

If completeness of affected-authority lookup is not itself established, advance a provider-wide nonwrapping support fence and dirty all authorities. This bounded scalar invalidation is not a claim that rescanning all affected groups is free. It is a safe first fallback in place of an unproven incremental dependency index. ## Paid full-scan support authorization

A support job names a finite immutable C, exact group member inventory M/root/version, exact desired ad/anchor for every member, the source's relevant current guards, and one admitted work/stock reservation. It cannot substitute a token-union count, namespace name or publisher-reported dependency list for these inputs.

Declare explicit ceilings for:

`e = all distinct records in the named inventory traversal, including selected, excluded, ineligible and tombstone records actually traversed`, `i = total token/posting record visits`, `m = actual group members`, `d = total complete local assessment/support dependency-record visits`, `p = actual physical posting/index-node visits`, and `b = total input bytes read, including repeated reads`.

Also admit output/temporary bytes, peak retained layouts/maps, primitive comparison/hash/decode work, maximum scan duration and the source-wide number of such jobs. Each STRUCT2 entry has at most 32 tokens, but dependencies and raw bytes need their own bounds. The number of distinct emitted-token incidences of the selected subset is at most 32e; **total visits i need not be**, because checks may reread records or traverse retained excluded postings. Exact posting checks use a retained finite layout traversal or bounded actual lookup paths, with their aggregate node visits p supplied explicitly. An index-root hash alone is not a posting-lookup algorithm. Unknown or excessive traversal cost gives nondecision; no O(1) membership access is presumed. The repeated m-by-e anchor-search reads are included in b and comparison work; a resident-cache optimization instead needs explicit RAM/allocation and complete-input preservation bounds, not free repeated access.

One conservative deterministic fallback is:

1. Validate the complete retained group map against its named root and exact local transaction history. Enumerate all m leaf records and their signed ads; visit at most `2m+1` Patricia nodes under the inherited canonical finite map invariant. If exact history/map reconstruction is unavailable, stop UNKNOWN; remote memberCount is insufficient.
2. Scan every entry of C in canonical entry order. Check the complete inventory's exact entry identifiers, typed descriptor/replay/target relationships, completed extraction metadata, selected-membership state and actual declared FIND postings. Traverse known excluded or known-ineligible records as such; they are not support witnesses and need not regain eligibility. An unrelated record whose native assessment is dirty is classified UNKNOWN_SUPPORT, without automatically making every group's support unknown. Visit at most e entry records and i posting records. Missing/incoherent inventory structure is different from one known record's unsupported native status: inability to establish the named inventory cut produces UNKNOWN_INVENTORY. A descriptor's token list is checked, not supplied by an LLM guess.
3. For each member in canonical membership order, scan at most e entry records to locate its **exact designated signed anchor chain**, not an arbitrary theorem sharing the token. For those actual group anchors, inspect the complete retained native-acceptance and current protection/disclosure/support dependencies, within the supplied total d dependency-record visits. Check the member's token against that entry's at-most-32-token list and its current guards. A definitely excluded/withdrawn/missing anchor is unsupported; incomplete actual-anchor dependencies give UNKNOWN_SUPPORT. Neither can authorize renewal. Unrelated ineligible or unknown-support entries do not obstruct renewal when no actual member relies on them and the inventory/posting traversal itself is coherent. This deliberately simple fallback costs up to `m*e` entry comparisons plus `32m` token comparisons; a faster dictionary requires its own admitted construction/preservation argument.
4. Construct a finite exact summary binding BindingID/A, supportFence, C, groupID/root/version, exact per-member ad/anchor identities, actual guard versions and the scan's completion/work record. Before publication of that summary, atomically compare the relevant supportFence and current roots/versions against the starting cut. Any intervening change discards its authorization value and gives STALE_INPUT. Partial scan output remains charged scratch, not a current summary.

The same traversal has a classification mode that emits the exact finite supported/unsupported/unknown member partition and its reasons. An explicitly withdrawn member is unsupported, not a reason to stop before identifying other invalid members. Missing coherent dependencies or exhausted work is UNKNOWN, never a completed all-supported summary. The baseline incident procedure pays for this complete classification pass before cleanup and a second complete authorization pass over the actual resulting map. The classifier does not make a wholly dirty group renewable.

All decoding, hashing, signature work, table reads, dependency inspection and I/O fit the supplied actual resource vector; abstract visit counts alone are not runtime bounds. For example, a valid upper-bound plan may charge

`Wscan <= Wdecode(b) + Wmap(2m+1) + Wentry(e) + Wposting(i,p) + Wdependency(d) + Wcompare(m*e+32m) + Wcommit`.

Every W term is an independently supplied conservative cost function or bounded implementation qualification target, not a measured fact in this note. Admission must supply numeric ceilings and a finite calendar before promising service. If a term has no credible bound, that operating case is not admitted. Reusing one scan across several groups requires an explicit exact common-input argument and separate member/anchor work, not multiplication-free reuse by assertion.

The existing native assessment for an actual required anchor may be reused only with its complete coherent exact replay/checker/foundation/footprint and current dependency guards under the native contract. If that required assessment is missing, stale or dirty, its member receives NEEDS_NATIVE_REVALIDATION/UNKNOWN_SUPPORT. An unrelated excluded or dirty inventory entry does not trigger required native revalidation for this group. Ordinary separately funded native rechecking, closure acquisition, retention and extraction may later restore a needed assessment. Those operations are not hidden inside Wscan or treated as free because the descriptor was once accepted. No native execution is performed by this research document.

The resulting local authorization rules are deliberately asymmetric:

- **PUT:** every introduced or replaced ad has a complete current exact support witness at authorization, plus ordinary owner/state/resource guards. An idempotent PUT submitted anew also needs current support. Other already-invalid group members need not regain support for this individual replacement to proceed; the group remains dirty until fully checked.
- **DROP:** authenticated exact owner/group, coherent expected version/root/ad CID, current disclosure to perform the operation and finite budget suffice. Neither the removed member nor residual members must regain native support. Partial destructive batches may remove several invalid members over time without deadlocking on still-invalid survivors.
- **CLOSE:** exact owner/state/disclosure/resource authority suffices; no theorem support is required to retire routing service. Independent custody pins remain protected.
- **RENEW:** every actual current member at the exact resulting root has a complete current support witness. All relevant guards must still match at the local authorization boundary. No dirty member may be carried through a newly authorized group renewal merely because some other member remains eligible.

All support authorization is a producer obligation. The holder authenticates and enforces operational state/capacity but does not fetch evidence or certify an honest producer's scan. A malicious provider can still publish false leads; independent native checking remains the mathematical trust boundary.

## Local authorization, current coverage and snapshot publication

One authoritative local transaction performs each new support-dependent authorization: compare its exact source support/owner/guard versions; consume the finite one-shot local action authority; persist the immutable planned call and expected state; then allow dispatch. An incident that wins this local ordering first blocks that authorization. An authorization that wins first is an old admitted action which might already be sent or claimed remotely. No local transaction is atomic with a foreign holder.

The conservative lane allows at most one unresolved **effectful group operation** under the exact source lane key `(holder,p,F,E,A)`, including a submitted OPEN before a groupID exists. This is at most one for its ACTIVE group and is stronger than permitting a separate lane for each attempted generation. OPEN reserves and persists that lane plus the possible new empty-group allocation before dispatch; it cannot be used as a harmless read that bypasses an unresolved PUT/RENEW/CLOSE. An operation's effect is resolved by its actual terminal committed/failed outcome, or coherent authenticated exact after-state already obtained through an authorized inherited reconciliation path that, together with the unique retained before/call history, proves that transition committed. The strict recipe does not create a second effectful observation call to obtain that state. A familiar root alone is insufficient: a RENEW changes version with unchanged root; a PUT can change expected ad CID. An invocation replay may return its existing outcome metadata under NET2 but does not create another execution or free reply-body delivery. No new generic outcome-fetch operation is invented here.

Effect resolution and terminal accounting differ. A known committed operation can release this serialization lane while original paid delivery/outcome records or reserved accounting still remain. Those liabilities stay in the action inventory until their inherited terminal/release conditions. An unknown timeout or missing outcome cannot release the lane. A conflicting old group generation fenced by actual expiry/replacement cannot regain ACTIVE status, but relying on that fallback generally forfeits the no-gap service promise. After a confirmed RENEW changes only version/lease, the source may perform a paid local summary rebinding to that exact new version only after rechecking the unchanged membership, anchor identities and current support fences; the old version-bound summary is not silently current.

Reconciliation is monotone with respect to known holder transitions, not “last message received wins.” A delayed SG_RESULT for an already reconciled old call may arrive after a newer call committed. Its lower-version observation settles only that old call's history/accounting and remains historical; it does not overwrite current group root/version, lease knowledge or support phase. An equal-version observation must agree with exact immutable bindings and coherent membership/root/count/bytes at that version. It cannot undo a separately established CLOSED/EXPIRED lifecycle gate or newer local support guard. Remaining-duration observations can legitimately differ at distinct observation times and remain historical; they are not directly comparable global deadlines. An incompatible same-version state or an unrecognized higher transition without coherent retained lineage gives FENCED_UNKNOWN and paid reconciliation, not a guessed overwrite. No delayed ACTIVE result resurrects an actually retired group generation, a dirty borrower cache or expired funding. Updating current state from a justified higher transition still requires current local guard checks before any SUPPORTED claim.

A borrower claims current coverage only after resolving the provider-key owner's current settled state and versions, the applicable A/provider support fence, its own current entry/posting eligibility, and actual required copy/path observations with ages. Unchanged namespace identity never validates a cached coverage bit. A coarse incident fence can temporarily dirty unaffected borrowers too; their repair work is explicitly counted. Invalidating one scalar is not a free recomputation of all borrower aggregates.

Index publication uses separate semantic and layout commits:

- Adding/removing/changing selected entries commits a new finite C and its exact FIND posting relation under current native/public/custody guards. Native PUBLIC_SERVE/custody prerequisites are unchanged. Routing may still be PENDING/PARTIAL; an index entry does not fabricate coverage.
- A pure compaction prepares finite output layout R', proves exact preservation of C's entry/descriptor/token/posting relation against still-current inputs, reserves old/new peak state, then atomically installs R'. It changes no A, ad, group root, native support or selected membership. Failure before commit leaves R authoritative and output charged until lawful cleanup.
- Existing FIND snapshots remain bound to exact predicate/extractor/planner, result-record schema, immutable index cut, order and original cursor limits. New snapshots use the new layout implementing C. No cursor is resumed against an unrelated layout or descriptor universe. Old readers retain their real roots/allocations; current-versus-historical delivery guards remain enforced.
- ROUTE_LIST snapshots remain F's exact holder-cut observations, with only ACTIVE/unexpired groups included at creation. Rows need not reveal group mode/A. Their historical row labels do not become present-time guarantees, and expiry or incident after capture does not rewrite delivered history.

## Incident state machine and quiescent repair

An incident is a finite locally authoritative support change, not simply an adversarial packet. Its first transition is local: increment the applicable support fence; dirty the affected current summaries/coverage; stop new PUT/RENEW authorizations in the affected group lane; record the bounded group repair cut and original deadline. Current public/native reliance on invalidated support stops under the native/disclosure gates. Existing custody and retention promises remain separate.

The group follows this deterministic sequence:

| From | Required transition | Disposition on missing prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| SUPPORTED / RENEWING | Invalidate first, record incident, enter DRAINING | If affected-set completeness is unknown, dirty the whole admitted authority/provider set and price that larger repair cut |
| DRAINING | Settle the one old effectful call; cancel only if the actual authoritative unclaimed-cancellation race is won | Keep uncertainty and charges; UNKNOWN_OUTCOME or deadline failure yields DEGRADED, never invented absence |
| DRAINING to CLASSIFYING | Bind actual post-settlement group root/version/map and ad CIDs; perform the first complete paid support classification scan | Unknown map/history yields FENCED_UNKNOWN; coherent actual expiry yields DEGRADED/retirement, not maintained service |
| CLASSIFYING to CLEANING | Freeze the exact invalid-member list and supported replacements, if any, under current guards; form actual encoded cleanup batches | Unknown support or stale input yields nondecision or a separately reserved bounded restart, not an invented repair plan |
| CLEANING | Execute bounded exact cleanup batches serially; each batch admits own full cost and expected-state outcome | Partial completed batches persist; invalid residual members do not block further DROP. Conflicts require bounded paid reconciliation, not automatic retry |
| CLEANING to CHECKING | All planned invalid memberships removed or actually replaced; bind the exact final map | Missing settlement stays dirty; local intended root is not the actual resulting root |
| CHECKING | Run the paid full supported-result scan and atomically validate its guards | Unsupported/unknown/stale/work-exhausted gives no renewal; native revalidation is a separate admitted job |
| CHECKING to RENEWING | Persist and dispatch one exact RENEW after current support/state/resource recheck | Insufficient conservative deadline margin gives DEGRADED before issuing a service promise |
| RENEWING to SUPPORTED | Reconcile successful exact new version/root/lease observation, then compare dispatched support/fence dependencies against current local guards and perform paid exact summary rebinding | If a later incident won, record the real remote renewal and spending but remain DIRTY/DRAINING for its repair obligation; failure/unknown remains DEGRADED or FENCED_UNKNOWN, never a restored current cache |

No fresh ordinary insertion, anchor replacement or scheduled renewal can bypass DRAINING/CLASSIFYING/CLEANING to restore an invalid member. Emergency repair is not an unbounded priority channel. It consumes a separately reserved finite source/holder service share and the ordinary one-shot authority. New unrelated work may proceed only where its joint reservations cannot steal the promised repair slots or invalidate the named stable repair inputs. A full repair queue cannot reject authoritative invalidation: advance the coarse fence and expose DEGRADED outside the admitted repair episode, while refusing the corresponding bounded availability promise. The fallback group inventory remains finite and charged; an unbounded new queue entry per incident is forbidden.

The positive procedure uses actual settlement of the old call, not global cancellation. A claimed pre-incident RENEW may commit after the incident, extending a stale lead with unchanged root and higher version. A claimed pre-incident PUT may change both membership and ad CID. Both are incorporated into the actual cleanup state. Stale leads are not allowed to become current native/public reliance. Quiescent withdrawal is reported only after all known restoring effects are terminal or actually effect-fenced, invalid rows are observed removed/expired/closed, and no new restoring authorization is live. Accounting/history can still remain charged after that report.

The simple service proof below requires the single outstanding call to settle within a supplied bound. It does not use an extra competing cleanup call to fence it. A future emergency-contender variant would require explicit additional outstanding-slot limits, exact higher-version fencing and joint accounting; it is not silently admitted here. Local timeout, merely issued DROP, local version increments or a hoped-for future expiry are not remote effect fences.

If additional authoritative incidents occur during scan or renewal authorization, their support fence wins or loses the same local ordering. A new incident can invalidate a completed scan. The supplied finite incident load must cover all such restarts/reclassification; otherwise the producer withholds renewal and reports lost service. No proof is made under unlimited adversarial incident rate.

In particular, an incident can occur **after** local RENEW authorization but **before** its successful result is reconciled. The result still updates actual holder version/root/lease, effect settlement and spend. It cannot overwrite the newer incident phase or restore SUPPORTED from the earlier scan. Only a current guarded comparison and valid rebind/rescan permits a new current borrower claim. Thus once-only result settlement does not become an implicit second authorization of its obsolete native/support assumptions.

## Joint cost and deadline admission

The admission input is the **actual finite affected group list** and all existing liabilities, not the assumption that repair succeeds. For a group g, let:

- `q_g` be its unsettled old-effect count, at most one; `o_g` its bounded settlement/delivery/reconciliation calls;
- `m_g,e_g,i_g,d_g,p_g,b_g` be its post-settlement scan ceilings, including any permitted old PUT's exact possible members and dependencies;
- `u_g` be invalid members to remove and `v_g` explicit supported replacements; no unspecified search for an alternative anchor is included;
- `B_g` be the number of actual encoded cleanup batches, each respecting both 64 mutations and 64 KiB including the new header;
- `a_g` be the admitted number of complete scan attempts, at least two for the baseline classification-plus-result procedure, and `r_g` the admitted number of renewal attempts, including bounded conflicts/restarts;
- `Z_g` be actual source/holder stock for old/new maps, snapshots, action outcomes, native/protection owners, summaries and repair state.

Unknown old action effects are priced over their explicit finite before/after possibilities. `B_g` is computed from the actual encoded planned mutations; `ceil((u_g+v_g)/64)` is only a lower bound when bytes force smaller batches. A conservative singleton plan has at most `u_g+v_g` batches when each admitted single-mutation request fits; if even one does not fit, that operation is unsupported under this binding. Any separate lookup/native recheck/alternative-anchor generation cost is added explicitly, or the repair plan uses DROP alone.

Admission also checks finite counter headroom: actual holder stateVersion plus every possible old-operation increment, cleanup change and admitted renewal increment must fit uint64 without wrap. Source support/owner versions and authority/action counters need their own complete planned headroom. A physically affordable episode that cannot perform its guarded state transitions before exhaustion is not admitted; counter exhaustion yields the explicit nondecision/re-admission branch rather than a successful service witness.

For resource vectors with componentwise inequality, reserve at least

`Qrepair = Qidentify + sum_g (Qsettle(g,o_g) + Qcleanup(g,B_g) + a_g*Qscan(g) + r_g*Qrenew(g) + QlocalCommit(g) + Qaftercare(g))`.

`Qidentify` includes the full paid authority/group enumeration when coarse invalidation requires it. Qsettle includes actual outcome delivery/observation work, not free recovery. Qcleanup includes encoding, signatures where relevant, transaction staging and all failed attempts declared in B_g. Qrenew includes the retained-byte-time extension and reply/control costs. Qaftercare covers funded old-history cleanup, remaining reservations and subsequent decision work; it does not promise release of foreign physical bytes at a source-selected instant.

Admit jointly:

`alreadySpent + unspentIncumbentReserve + unspentRepairReserve + unspentOptionalReserve <= currentGrant`;

`currentChargedStock + repairPeakOverlap <= eachActualStockCap`.

The complete Qrepair plan is initially covered by unspentRepairReserve; as actual work is spent, the spent counter increases and the corresponding unspent reservation decreases. These residual reservations are mutually disjoint and exclude already-spent work. Aftercare is a disjoint component of its named reserve, not counted a second time under incumbent work. A completed prefix cannot remain in an unspent reserve, and lost outcomes retain the inherited conservative charge rather than replenishing budget. This is the same repaired grant-accounting discipline as the [continuation contract](../reciprocal-operability/continuation-admission-contract.md); it is not a new grant per plan or per stock observation.

The stock inequality uses actual current charged state plus positive peak increments; all planned release credits are zero. At an actually completed local release barrier, remeasure stock and admit a successor from that new cut, without subtracting the old release again. A stock recut never resets spent work, disjoint residual reservations, uncertainty or live obligations. Foreign expiry/CLOSE or a source's intended deletion is not observed foreign physical reclamation.

The inequalities apply per actual owner/node and every resource dimension, not just aggregate network sums. Holder admission remains independent; a source reserve is not a holder grant. Actual unknown or unreclaimed bytes remain charged. E-style OWNER_RELEASE_REQUIRED custody is not a lease expired by the end of this incident frame. No future grant, foreign GC acknowledgment or unrestricted resource market is presumed.

Deadline admission additionally supplies bounded primitive durations and an explicit slot list on available executors. For a serialized one-group episode, a sufficient bound is

`Tidentify + Tsettle + Tcleanup + sum_{j=1..a_g} Tscan_j + sum_{j=1..r_g} Trenew_j + Tcommit + TguardSlack < remainingSafeLeaseMargin`.

Multiple groups use a concrete finite calendar with per-group precedence edges and incumbent work in the same slots; total CPU or call count alone does not prove deadlines. The dispatcher uses earliest safe deadline, then original incident age, then exact groupID; new arrivals cannot reset ages. A sufficient admission method is conservative serial scheduling on one actually reserved executor, summing every predecessor's worst-case duration before each group's deadline. More parallelism may be separately justified. Refusing an infeasible calendar is safe, not positive service evidence.

Remote leases are holder-local. This contract does not subtract an observed remaining duration from an unrelated global clock. One permissible **additional operating assumption** is known bounded relative clock rate: over the relevant no-restart interval holder elapsed time is at most `(1+rho)` times source monotonic elapsed time. Before any effectful dispatch can escape, retain the exact NET invocation occurrence, call commitment, holder/group/authority binding, local monotonic time-origin identity and a timestamp `s0` of that invocation's **original effectful dispatch**, or a conservatively earlier local timestamp proved to precede its possible commit. Repeated identical arguments under a fresh invocation are not the same occurrence; an outcome from one cannot supply another's lease basis.

If that exact invocation's authentic commit-boundary outcome reports remaining duration L, an expiration lower bound is `s0 + L/(1+rho)` in the same continuous source monotonic origin, because its original commit cannot precede s0. A later retransmission, exact-action replay or delayed outcome delivery may return the old immutable result: its later send/receive time **must not replace s0 or refresh the bound**. Use the persisted original-dispatch or proven-earlier basis, never whichever request happened to retrieve the result. Every relevant elapsed interval must satisfy the stated rate assumption and continuous source/holder lease-clock recovery conditions. Missing occurrence history, an uncertain original-dispatch basis, discontinuous monotonic origin or unknown rate yields UNKNOWN_DEADLINE; an expired/insufficient computed margin yields no no-gap service promise. Fresh actual renewal can establish a new separately bound observation, but replay cannot. Wire observations retain their historical semantics.

Bounded response/settlement durations, willing holder admission, stable surviving support during the final scan/renewal window, and the stated finite incident load are primitive service premises. They must be independently falsifiable. The conclusion “the invalid member is removed and the survivor renewed in time” is **not** among the premises.

For a particularly conservative complete episode, let W be the old unresolved-effect count (W at most one), settlement require at most h ordinary control/delivery actions and s elapsed time, and let N bound the complete post-settlement members, including any reserved prior PUT delta. Let D bound the actual complete assessment/guard dependency input together with the separately bounded e/i/p/byte fields. The first completed classification must exhaustively partition the **actual coherent post-settlement membership** as `Mpost = Keep disjoint-union Remove`: every member of Keep has complete current support for its exact ad/anchor and all required guards; every member of Remove is definitely unsupported under that same current cut and has separately authorized routing removal. No actual member is omitted, and this simple episode admits no member with UNKNOWN_SUPPORT. In particular, one fully supported promised survivor does not justify retaining a different member whose acceptance/dependency record is unknown.

The second full scan must establish complete current support for **every actual member** of the resulting map, whose membership is exactly Keep after the planned deletions. The stable-guard and primitive-scan premises guarantee this for the named complete inputs; “both scans finished within time” by itself does not. The promised incident-cut survivors belong to Keep. Here “unsupported” is an operational support/publication disposition, not a proof that the mathematical statement is false. If classification produces UNKNOWN_SUPPORT, this simple service theorem does not apply: separately admitted native revalidation or a separately specified owner-authorized conservative removal plan must supply its actual work, remaining membership and deadline. Such a branch is not hidden inside the original i or allowed to omit the unknown row.

Suppose each of these complete scans takes at most `S(N,D)`, the entire chosen cleanup complement has `i = |Remove|` members, each singleton DROP takes at most d time, one RENEW takes at most r, and all identification/local commits/rebinding take at most l. No replacement, retry, unknown-support revalidation or additional relevant guard edit is included in this particular episode.

For any survivor whose continuity is promised **from the incident cut**, every admitted possible prior-action outcome must preserve the ACTIVE group lifecycle and that survivor's exact row under the same owner, with the conservative lease margin used below. The original lease must cover the settlement prefix even if the old operation declines. A prior CLOSE, expiry, or DROP/replacement removing that promised survivor violates this premise. Such an outcome can support a smaller post-settlement service claim after new admission, but cannot backdate continuity. In the positive two-member trace the sole prior action is RENEW, which preserves membership/lifecycle on successful commit; coherent timely failure leaves the still-active original membership through the admitted margin.

Under these additional row-preservation conditions,

`L = W*s + 2*S(N,D) + i*d + r + l`

is a sufficient serial episode duration. The number of **new cleanup mutations plus renewals** is at most `i+1`; the old settlement work of at most `W*h` calls/actions is additional, as are all scans and accounting costs. Singleton DROP is a conservative batch plan only after each exact encoded single-member request is known to fit the control ceiling.

For a frozen set of at most Q dirty groups admitted in the actual deadline/age order above, each group's safe margin must exceed its own preceding service-prefix sum. `Q*max_g L_g` is a sufficient but often loose common margin. The proof is induction through a concrete reserved serial slot list: each primitive bound completes its named predecessor, the two scans produce the actual cleanup classification and exact resulting support, and the final admitted RENEW precedes the conservative old-service deadline. Extra guard changes, failed primitive supply, missing outcomes or scan-bound violations are explicit failed premises, not an unbounded retry loop inside L.

The theorem's service object is narrowly the **survivor's actual row and containing lease at the specified holder, without an expiry gap**. Coarse source invalidation can make even a valid survivor's current coverage assertion UNKNOWN throughout classification/repair. The theorem is not uninterrupted current-coverage reporting, uninterrupted native/public disclosure, query completion, four-copy overlap or unfamiliar-reader reachability. Those stronger end-to-end services require their separate guards, markers, copies, provider availability and resource calendars. A later equal-service comparison must report this temporary assertion degradation and may not trade it away invisibly.

## Two-member success trace

Use one actual holder h, one new-mode group under A, and exact members `a(x)` and `b(y)`. Both x and y have independently completed native acceptance, retained permitted closure, completed extraction, exact public descriptors and actual FIND postings. Their group root is R0 at version 8. A second local index run borrows b through the current provider-key owner. This is a routing/support trace; no native theorem or runtime result is fabricated by the letters x/y.

1. A paid compaction changes the two local runs to one exact representation of the same C. Old readers retain their allocations. A, ads, root R0 and remote rows remain unchanged; this step requires no remote mutation.
2. Before the incident, an exact RENEW at `(8,R0)` is legitimately authorized and claimed remotely but unresolved. A local incident withdraws x's public/support eligibility. The source advances its support fence and marks current coverage dirty before any new authorization. y's mathematics remains eligible, but its group coverage waits for repair.
3. The already claimed RENEW commits at version 9 with R0 unchanged and delivers its retained exact result within the admitted settlement bound. The source records actual old/new lease observations and pays remaining result/history costs. This stale a lead is not renewed by a new post-incident authorization.
4. The first paid complete classification scan of the actual `(9,R0)` map identifies a as unsupported and b as supported under the new guards. An authenticated paid DROP at `(9,R0)` names the exact current a ad CID. It does not require x, a, or other members to be currently supported. It commits version 10/root R1 containing exactly `b(y)`.
5. The post-DROP support scan visits the exact remaining map and bounded current inventory, establishes y's exact anchor/token/posting/assessment guards, and binds its summary to `(A,currentFence,10,R1)`. Any still-selected ineligible x record is explicitly excluded from the current public selection cut or marked ineligible and cannot witness support; historical/native custody records may remain independently retained.
6. The writer rechecks all guards and authorizes RENEW at `(10,R1)`. The successful commit gives version 11, the same R1 and a new finite lease observation. The borrower's current coverage can now be recomputed under the new fence and actual remaining placement/path observations.

There is one old settlement, one pre-cleanup classification scan, one single-member cleanup batch, one post-cleanup complete scan and one new renewal. Suppose their independently supplied duration ceilings, plus identification/commits, are respectively `t_old,t_classify,t_drop,t_scan,t_renew,t_local`; if their sum is below the conservative old-service deadline margin and their complete Q/Z vectors fit alongside incumbent work, the stated sequence renews b before that margin. The proof is summing the admitted serialized predecessor bounds and applying each exact transition's postcondition. It does not assume the target repair result as an input.

The trace may be instantiated by declaring, for example, six reserved duration slots of one unit each and a seven-unit conservative margin, with a finite local no-new-incident window and sufficient supplied vector resources. Those units are a symbolic service fixture, not seconds, observed capacity or an empirical claim. The general inequalities, not that favorable fixture, govern larger groups.

## Defeating and boundary traces

**No settlement before deadline.** Keep the same x/y incident, but the claimed old renewal's effect remains unknown beyond b's safe service margin. The strict lane cannot dispatch a second effectful call by assuming timeout means cancellation. Source current safety holds, but no-gap survivor service is defeated under this supply. A later admitted repair can restore service without retroactively calling the gap maintained.

**Wrong old state after PUT.** Replace the old renewal with a pre-incident supported PUT replacing a's signed ad by a higher revision. It later commits. DROP using the pre-incident expected CID or root fails atomically. Repair must use the actual reconciled map/CID and charge the failed attempt if its calendar allowed one; silently ignoring this difference invalidates the service bound.

**Two unsupported members across batches.** If a and b are both invalid and the actual byte/work bound permits only one DROP per batch, requiring full residual support before the first DROP deadlocks. This candidate authorizes each destructive batch from ownership/expected state, so it can remove a while b remains invalid, then remove b. The group remains DIRTY and cannot renew until the exact remaining map is fully supported; an empty result may be closed with its real history counted.

**Scan beyond allocation.** y remains mathematically valid, but its complete dependency inventory or the `m*e` fallback exceeds the admitted d/b/work/duration bound. The scan yields WORK_EXHAUSTED/NEEDS_NATIVE_REVALIDATION as appropriate, not a partial all-supported certificate. Withholding renewal is safe and defeats the promised survivor service for that allocation. Calling the index “compact” cannot lower these actual costs by definition.

**Incident during scan.** A guard changes after the last inspected record but before summary publication. The fence comparison fails. A bounded extra scan succeeds only when its full cost and no-gap calendar were already admitted; unlimited restarts do not inherit the original promise.

**Expiry mistaken for observation.** After the lane's prior effect is lawfully settled, a paid matching OPEN sent to observe/reopen an old group arrives after its expiry and creates a separately admitted empty fresh generation. The source reserves the `(holder,A)` lane and possible new generation before dispatch, records this as empty/new rather than R0 recovery, and retains old possible state/history charges. Such OPEN is outside the strict positive outcome-settlement recipe; an unresolved old action cannot be bypassed by calling it read-only. Any repopulation is paid new placement with an actual service gap unless other real copies preserve the separate multi-copy claim.

**Pure compaction that loses a posting.** Token unions match but the new layout omits `(y,b)`. Exact semantic preservation fails; no zero-remote-compaction claim legitimizes the missing FIND relation. The new layout is not published as equivalent.

**Generation rollback.** An old source backup submits an old expected version or tries to recreate A with a reset serial. Recovery fences uncertain authority/spend instead. A holder's late old-GC cursor remains bound to the old group/storage generation and cannot delete a replacement row. Fresh identities do not erase old outcome/custody liabilities.

**Late result after later progress.** A committed version-9 RENEW was reconciled from coherent exact after-state, releasing its effect lane while its original reply remained pending. A later DROP commits version 10/root R1. When the original version-9 result arrives, it may settle its own retained delivery/accounting but cannot replace version 10/R1 by version 9/R0 or restore a dirty support phase. Equal roots would not change this rule: version and lineage, not arrival order, determine historical reconciliation.

**Replay does not restart a lease clock.** With equal-rate continuous clocks, an original call is dispatched at source time 0, commits at time 1 and records ten units remaining; its actual lease expires at time 11. An exact replay sent at time 20 returns that old immutable outcome. Substituting replay-send time 20 would falsely infer a lower expiry bound of 30. The retained original basis gives only `0+10=10`, already past at replay delivery. The old result may settle history but supplies no current margin. If the original basis is missing, the result yields UNKNOWN_DEADLINE instead of a fresh ten-unit lease.

**A supported survivor does not cover an unknown neighbor.** The actual coherent group has supported b and c with UNKNOWN_SUPPORT; no member is classified definitely unsupported. The classification scan finishes within its bound with those statuses. Even a separately paid second scan could finish within its bound and reproduce the same unknown result; the ordinary state machine instead stops at classification nondecision. Taking i=0 and merely adding two scan durations to L would still leave c in the proposed renewed root, so the RENEW support gate forbids the operation. The exhaustive known-support partition premise fails. The source retains DIRTY/DEGRADED status and must separately admit c's revalidation or a complete owner-authorized removal plan; it cannot omit c, call UNKNOWN false, or claim the original episode preserved the promised service.

## Exact costs needed for the later adoption decision

An equal-resource comparison must supply the same finite keys, selected targets/incidences, actual holder placements, group capacities, support/incident workload, copy/path service, native/custody duties and new arrivals for each alternative. It must price:

- Initial/adoption DROP/PUT/OPEN/CLOSE and all uncertainty/peak history, separately from later steady-state savings.
- Ordinary actual group/marker renewal, distinct-holder dispersion and existing empty/partial groups; `min(M,rU)` bounds selected nonempty groups only.
- Paid C/R construction, exact preservation scans, summaries, member/anchor and native dependency checks, borrower resolution, coarse-invalidation blast radius and repair admission slots.
- New-token placement, changed anchors, holder churn, capacity-driven namespace partition/transfers, skipped/refused input and service gaps.
- Incident settlement/cleanup/survivor renewal calendars under identical primitive supplies, including the loss of batching under changed header sizes and actual ad bytes.
- All old/new physical layouts/maps, snapshots, durable histories, outcomes and owner-required retained evidence; no foreign physical-GC observation invented from local arithmetic.

The baseline full scan is intentionally simple and may be too costly at large B. That is a falsifiable possible reason to reject or refine it, not an excuse to assume a free incremental summary. A later exact incremental mechanism can be compared to this complete fallback after its invalidation/persistence properties are independently checked.

In particular, selected-entry additions advance this baseline's coarse support fence and can dirty all of A's group summaries. Independently rescanning e retained records for G dispersed groups can cost proportional to `G*e` entry work, before repeated member/anchor searches and actual dependency/I/O costs. Removing remote compaction mutations does not remove this possibly dominant renewal-authorization expense. Batching additions into one guarded finite C transition may reduce how often it occurs, but creates its own publication delay/peak-state tradeoff and is not a selected free optimization here. The comparison must count actual dirty intervals and rescans at the same service level rather than compare a fully maintained old profile against a silently weaker candidate.

## Remaining obligations and handoff

This candidate closes abstract local transition choices left open by the architecture: identity/nonreuse discipline; distinct symbolic dispatch; exact support inputs and paid fallback; asymmetric destructive cleanup; conservative one-unresolved-effect sequencing; the pre-incident race; exact resulting-root renewal; borrower/index/snapshot distinctions; and finite joint service inequalities with a positive and defeating trace.

It does **not** allocate real protocol identifiers, select a profile, qualify a parser/storage implementation, instantiate a large equal-resource comparison, supply actual primitive cost/timing bounds, establish native bootstrap feasibility, measure useful growth or authorize execution. Those are remaining work, not implied findings. Independent review must challenge dispatch completeness, crash/race cases, full support-dependency coverage and the service proof before root integrates any result. Existing architecture/source cuts remain unchanged.
