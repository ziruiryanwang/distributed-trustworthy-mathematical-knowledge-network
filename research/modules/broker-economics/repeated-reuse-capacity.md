# Repeated checked reuse: concentrated demand and paid source capacity

Date: 2026-09-20. Author: `bounded_domains_author`. Status: **HELD complete draft for independent resource and service review; conditional comparison, no new mechanism selected**. Concept/specification and document arithmetic only. No implementation, executable model, native extraction, Lean execution or benchmark.

## Decision and scope

Additional metadata capacity cannot discharge an evidence-source bottleneck. Existing I/E/native roles can nevertheless support a finite repeated-use offer by preparing another genuinely authorized native source before demand arrives. This can relocate service capacity while **increasing** total transferred evidence and independent checking. The decisive premises are exact missing evidence, actual per-owner residual grants, preparation time, current discoverability and receiver work, not the count of broker rows or replicas.

This note supplies one retained-cut example: four independent later readers, an overloaded sole admitted source path, and a funded core-qualified but target-cold replica that restores the offered service. It does not measure useful growth, show that four applications are novel mathematics, or prove perpetual availability. The [commissioning cycle](../../cycles/2026-09-20-reuse-capacity.md) owns scope and review. The preserved [matched-reuse comparison](matched-reuse-comparison.md), **55,511 bytes / `E95D8CEEA383A46A5A1A1C64AC47D103D7ACAC85375C331F772B42C789804D98`**, supplies the checked P-to-C-to-D path and native acquisition obligations. Its reviews do not certify this new repeated-service case.

The selected [V6 integration](../../../spec/OPERATIONAL-CORPUS-V6.md), [I exact bindings](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001I-direct-broker-bindings.md), [I economics](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001I-broker-economics-and-availability.md), [E custody](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001E-custody-contract.md), [G retrieval](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001G-structural-retrieval.md), and [F producer/client path](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001F-lean-producer-client-path.md) are inherited. ## Necessary bounds are not sufficient schedules

For owner o at this cut and resource coordinate a, let `G[o,a]` be the unchanged applicable grant and let `B[o,a]` account for actual already spent work plus disjoint reservations for every remaining incumbent obligation. A new admission must satisfy, at every prefix,

`spent[o,a] + remaining_incumbent[o,a] + remaining_new[o,a] <= G[o,a]`.

`R[o,a] = G[o,a] - B[o,a]` is conservatively allocatable residual only when the underlying bound is supplied for the actual owner and cut. For an upper certificate B, this difference is not an upper bound on all physically unused capacity; a cut impossibility needs an actual eligible service cap. A loose previous upper bound equal to the parent yields no guaranteed residual. Totals across owners cannot substitute for this inequality. Stock additionally needs actual existing allocations plus all overlapping new native environments, pins, staging, snapshots, versions and reply/control residue below each owner's caps until **actual release**. Calendar feasibility needs admitted queue/service intervals, source authority, acquisition frontiers, checking and settlement before their respective deadlines.

For readers whose missing payload can arrive only through source set S before a cutoff, a necessary cut bound is

`sum(reader missing bytes forced across S) + replica preparation bytes forced across S <= sum(eligible residual delivered-byte capacity of S)`.

Count repeated direct delivery to distinct receivers separately; paid relays or alternative source scopes would alter the cut. Use only receiving jobs whose bytes must actually be delivered by S in the specified policy: after a new replica is ready, its reader deliveries are not also charged to the old donor merely because that donor supplied the replica. Multi-source range acquisition needs an actual feasible assignment of eligible bytes, not arbitrary allocation of a whole theorem to a favorable owner. Similar necessary bounds apply to forced attempts, CPU and I/O when genuine lower costs are known. A conservative 12-MiB stage reservation is **not** such a lower cost. Aggregate cut bounds ignore indivisible jobs, causal frontiers, source/receiver overlap and deadlines, so they are not sufficient by themselves.

In particular, two sources can each have one feasible whole-job slot while A can use either and B can use only L. A greedily taking L defeats B despite the feasible assignment A-to-X, B-to-L. This is a counterexample to a universal greedy guarantee, not a proposal for a global scheduler. The local policy below obtains its conditional guarantee only from the supplied common eligibility, bounded contact set, separated arrivals and reserved service intervals.

The eight-call premise comes from complete item/frontier geometry, not from dividing 9 MiB by a payload ceiling. No executable frontier or Lean object has been instantiated here.

Use public exact-root read scopes, not an invented long-lived whole-replay lease. Actual byte transfer, the full causal object/inventory frontier, source admission/queue, source/reader setup, scopes, finite failure settlement and reader-side collection complete within 40 seconds once the native acquisition stage begins. The local policy has no knowledge of a network-wide optimal assignment. ## Matched defeat and what the replica really buys

In the **direct-only** alternative the same X owner/resources remain available but unexercised, so X never becomes an authorized evidence source. L is the cohort's sole admitted incoming path. Four readers each actually missing 9 MiB force at least **36 MiB** through L, exceeding its **32-MiB total residual bidirectional traffic capacity** even before control/framing. All four cannot finish by their unchanged deadlines under this service graph. This is a genuine necessary cut failure, not a reservation-policy artifact. Additional broker queries, perfect ranking or stronger identity cannot supply those bytes.

In this direct-only policy alternative L can assign the same two new stage grants to its first two admitted readers rather than to X and one reader. The selected 12-MiB-stage policy can admit only two direct stages alongside its control reserve. Its inability to admit a third from those conservative full-stage envelopes does **not** prove that three actual deliveries are physically impossible: three times the actual 9-MiB payload is only 27 MiB, and actual overhead/service may differ. Conversely the four-reader cut defeat remains even if the stage reservation is tightened. This separation is necessary when interpreting any alleged throughput limit.

In the replication alternative L supplies one X preparation stage and one direct-reader stage, fitting its 25-MiB complete new account including control. X supplies three funded reader stages. Counting one delivery of each required native payload byte before repeated reads or retries, direct completion of four readers would require 36 MiB; the replica route requires **45 MiB**: 9 MiB into X plus 36 MiB into readers. Repetition and complete control/framing costs remain additionally charged inside the supplied upper envelopes. The route also adds X's independent native checking, custody, public preparation, listing and aftercare. This is a transfer of service burden away from L, **not aggregate work saving** or a free replica. Endpoint accounting charges both sender and receiver as appropriate; 45 MiB counts delivered payload once per transfer, not the sum of both endpoints' traffic meters.

For identical missing size m, n readers, k readers assigned to a newly prepared source and donor preparation cost p, the donor's payload load changes from `n*m` to `p+(n-k)*m` under the same eligible paths. Relief requires `k*m > p`; network-wide payload increases by p if readers otherwise receive the same bytes. In this case p=m=9 MiB and k=3, so L's payload falls from36 to18 MiB while aggregate payload rises from36 to45 MiB. Full resource decisions still use actual per-owner vectors, public-service readiness and deadlines; this scalar comparison neither grants k slots nor proves any total CPU advantage.

A **foundation-cold** prospective X is a different input, not the demonstrated core-qualified case. Under the same restricted donor graph, it additionally lacks the actual 2-GiB core, so at least 2057 MiB of complete native payload would have to cross L's 32-MiB residual cut before X could independently qualify and serve. That preparation cannot fit. The conclusion is donor/path-specific: another original source with a newly funded compatible core scope, genuinely existing byte cache, larger real grant or earlier funded preparation could alter it. Already present core bytes without compatible assessment reduce transfer, not necessarily checking or time.

Likewise a replica that completes qualification/publication/listing only after a reader's usable acquisition window contributes no capacity for that deadline. An unlisted source is not reached by this two-lead policy; an old immutable I page does not learn it retrospectively. A source can remain an excellent future investment while failing this finite offer. Independent failure-domain benefits require separate evidence; another process label is not such evidence.

## Limits, reopening trigger and handoff

Local admission can enforce finite source-stage counts, actual resource ceilings, two-lead attempt bounds, expiry and safe stopping. It cannot enforce a worldwide arrival rate, guarantee that all independent readers see the new lead, or make one selfish/overloaded route globally optimal. The four arrival times, current common service graph, complete successful snapshots and bounded primitive service are external offer assumptions. More concentrated or simultaneous arrivals need actual queue/stock/deadline analysis; the cumulative byte bound remains necessary but cannot establish those temporal conditions.

Reopen this comparison when the specified offer changes or fails: a real original-source support path becomes admissible; actual missing bytes/native work differ; a replica lacks a qualified core; preparation/listing misses a deadline; the four-lead limit omits a needed source; source/reader stock or release time binds; or competing jobs invalidate the supplied local policy. First test existing eligible sources, genuine earlier preparation and separately funded native replicas against the unchanged offer. Do not infer a need for a new broker protocol from an unfunded donor, slow receiving checker or an unfortunate greedy assignment.

The concept deliverable is this owner-specific capacity distinction, scoped hotspot defeat and one complete conditional existing-role remedy. Meaningful long-term mathematical growth, superiority to local rediscovery and scalable decentralized load distribution remain separate empirical/design questions. All six native and eight reciprocal execution gates remain **NOT RUN**. The source is held for both complete independent reviews; the author does not certify its own service/accounting, change predecessor files or close the whole-project goal.
