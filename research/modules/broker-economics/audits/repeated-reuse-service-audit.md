## The necessary source-cut argument

**No finding against the stated necessary bound.** Under direct-only admission, each of four distinct readers must receive its actual nine-MiB missing canonical payload from L. Ordinary per-recipient deliveries and no additional permitted relay make the forced delivered payload at least `4*9=36 MiB`. L's owner fixes the entire new-cohort bidirectional traffic grant at **32 MiB** in both alternatives. Hence all four cannot complete under this graph and cap, even before control or framing is added.

This argument uses the explicitly fixed service cap, not merely `64 MiB parent - 32 MiB old upper certificate`. An upper bound on old consumption alone would not prove an upper bound on all possible residual service. The source supplies the actual separate cap and expressly forbids silently enlarging it by reclaiming old overreservation or advancing a funding interval.

The source also avoids the invalid inference `3*12 > 32`, where 12 MiB is a conservative whole-stage reservation. Three actual nine-MiB payloads total only 27 MiB; a selected two-stage policy is not a physical impossibility proof for three completions. Tightening the reservation could affect three-reader admission without fixing the four-reader actual lower bound.

After X is prepared, reader deliveries from X are not charged to L again simply because L previously supplied X. The donor payload changes from `n*m` to `p+(n-k)*m`, with relief only if `k*m>p`. For `p=m=9 MiB,n=4,k=3`, L's new delivered payload is 18 MiB, while total delivered payload across transfers rises from 36 to 45 MiB. This is capacity relocation with extra copying/checking, not aggregate work saving. Endpoint meter sums and payload counted once per transfer remain different quantities.
