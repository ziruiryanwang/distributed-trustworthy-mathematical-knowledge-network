### Later local selection is narrower than structural retrieval

For this supplied ordinary unreduced native-type family, T uses bounded syntactic direct-application matching: one substitution for a theorem's external parameters must consistently instantiate every occurrence in both equality operands to the stated intermediate query. The shared B, operation and equality identities are fixed, while T's local x, y, z, w, v remain distinct rigid variables. No answer ID is a selection input. This is a finite local candidate-selection policy on the acquired types, not unrestricted unification, proof search, a new G predicate or a mathematical acceptance rule.

C directly matches with parameter tuple `(x*y,z,w,v)`. For P, matching its left operand `(a*b)*c` to `(((x*y)*z)*w)*v` forces the raw syntactic assignment `a=(x*y)*z, b=w, c=v`. Its right operand is then `((x*y)*z)*(w*v)`, not the required raw term `(x*y)*(z*(w*v))`. Thus P fails this parameter-coherent **syntactic** direct-application test even though its independent-WILD retrieval pattern can pass. This does not say P is useless, cannot derive the intermediate by multiple applications, or cannot match after native definitional computation for a particular B and operation. No general native nonconversion result is asserted. Failure of this bounded heuristic test is neither a mathematical refutation nor a substitute for T's later complete P/C checking and actual proof-application checking.
