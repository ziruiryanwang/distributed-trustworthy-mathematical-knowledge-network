# Owner-local admission and paid normalization after a growth epoch

Date: 2026-09-20. Status: DRAFT for independent review; not selected. Author: `continuation_admission_author`. Concept/specification only. No implementation, executable model, extraction, Lean run or benchmark was performed. All six native and eight reciprocal qualification gates remain NOT RUN.

This note supplies an owner-local decision procedure, not a new wire protocol, global task list, capacity market or mathematical authority. Its starting evidence is [P's growth epoch](growth-epoch-contract.md), frozen source beginning `6D6B9F51`, selected by [CP-001P](../../../notes/checkpoints/CP-001P-joint-growth-epoch.md). It uses [O's published-batch normalization](batch-normalization-contract.md), [H's shared ownership and supplied slot](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001H-resource-supply-and-discovery.md), and the existing E/F/G custody, guarded publication and operation lifecycles through those sources. ## Question, result and non-result

P leaves provider B with 480 selected targets in 24 distinct twenty-target pending FeatureScopes. At most 15,360 keys have 61,440 selected four-copy rows. The exact retained replay root r0 and its support are not an extra selected B index target. Custody contains r0 and the 480 admitted replay roots. P supplies at most 196,608 calls/day, 28,800 CPU-seconds/day, 1 GiB metadata, 8 GiB RAM, 16 GiB disk, 8 GiB traffic/day and 32 GiB I/O/day, together with explicit count, runtime, source and holder premises. Those are independent B resources, not A's unused allowance.

Appending another twenty-target pending scope every day is not a continuation policy. It reaches 33 pending inputs on day 33 without normalization. More seriously, if each row uses its own holder/group, fewer FeatureScopes do not reduce group count: the same four copies of every key still need maintenance. An invocation must therefore account for normalization debt, group/row stock and current upkeep, not merely available target slots.

The proposed answer has three deliberately separate claims:

- A bounded, conservative, locally checkable admission procedure protects current duties and preserves actual state across finite frames. Its accept/reject decision is computed from finite counters, supplied upper envelopes and a concrete action ordering; it is not an oracle for arbitrary feasible scheduling.
- A positive same-supply frame from P's actual endpoint normalizes two old twenty-target scopes into completed sizes 32 and 8, and then admits four additional distinct targets in a size-four run. Its maximum call reservation is 196,100, without assuming group coalescence or additional hardware. The remaining 22 old pending scopes are still present.
- These facts do **not** establish that the offered twenty-target/day cadence is sustainable, that every useful target can be admitted, or that mathematical usefulness improves. Conditional backlog drainage and acceptance of some new work are weaker than efficient continuing growth. The explicit bottleneck below remains an engineering finding, not a defect disguised as success.

Independent agents nominate whatever mathematics they choose. Human intention, a shared root goal, popularity or a public unanswered-task queue is absent from this procedure. Local utility scores may order optional nominations; they cannot alter native acceptance or manufacture retention resources.

## The state carried between decisions

A **frame** is a finite owner-authorized plan with actual start cut, end-of-work cut, funded aftercare suffix and decision deadline. It is an internal planning object. Its identifier is not a new network schema or proof of capacity. Record the following receiver-owned data under the existing local single-writer/version boundaries:

| Component | Exact information that survives a frame boundary |
|---|---|
| Mathematical state | Foundation/extractor and qualified profile; semantic target identities; chosen native replay/descriptor bindings and eligibility; exact complete custody references; actual current disclosure authority |
| Logical index state | Disjoint target partition; completed dyadic tiers; pending/ready scopes in a stable local admission order; one possible partial split/carry lineage; exact emitted features and postings |
| Provider ownership | Unique provider/key owner entry, its transition/version, eligible anchor, external borrowers and actual old/new row bindings; no copied independent per-slot ledger |
| Physical state | All current, empty, retiring, closed and uncertain groups/rows; retained immutable scope records; maps, snapshots, export and custody pins; outcomes, fences, spend ledgers and required summaries |
| Work state | Actual completed steps and spent grants; unresolved replies; remaining plan cursor; cumulative old-pool normalization debt; reserved service/repair/cleanup work; obligations whose next due times lie beyond the work cut |
| Supply | Exact local count/byte/residency/CPU/I/O/traffic/call grants and clocks; actual external willingness/leases/admission; supplied operation completion and lawful cleanup envelopes, each explicitly conditional |

A day boundary resets none of the first five rows. A new budget is available only from a genuinely distinct owner grant. Expiry of a grant is neither successful normalization nor custody release. In particular, an E scope that requires owner-authorized release remains protected or visibly `OWNER_RELEASE_REQUIRED` under E; a frame cannot invent a custody TTL.

The end-of-work cut includes a reserved aftercare suffix through the current grant's terminal decision deadline. This suffix covers every retained obligation due in that interval, including successful new publication and interrupted transition states admitted by the frame's failure envelope. The next decision occurs while this suffix is still funded. If another grant is absent, the node stops further optional admissions and records the unresolved ongoing supply requirement. A finite suffix is not a proof of indefinite future upkeep. Existing owner release/reference rules still govern all retained custody; the absence of future supply does not authorize deletion or an inaccurate availability claim.

## Paid inspection before public selection

Three distinct predicates must not collapse:

1. **Native validity:** the complete selected native path independently accepts a particular formal judgment under exact foundations and explicit allowed axioms.
2. **Local nomination:** the local agent would like to retain or advertise that judgment. A frequency score, LLM estimate or personal mathematical investigation may inform this choice without entering the proof checker.
3. **Funded selection:** this owner has admitted the actual custody, disclosure, index, routing, upkeep and history work for a finite operational commitment. A valid nominated object can fail this predicate.

Before a result's full feature/evidence costs are known, only a separately paid inspection grant is admissible. It binds a finite request, byte/count/CPU/time ceilings, supported source endpoints, local staging, reference protection and its disposition authority. Inspect at most one proposed cohort at a time in this small policy, with at most twenty targets and 32 extracted tokens per successfully inspected target under the selected STRUCT2 profile. Missing support, longer closure traversal, expensive native reconstruction or a dirty guard can exhaust the inspection grant. The result is incomplete inspection or an ordinary native verdict; it is not permission to publish unchecked bytes.

Temporary evidence protection and failed work consume the same actual local supply. A rejection from public selection does not retroactively reject a valid theorem. The owner may retain it under another explicit custody grant, return its exact status to its local submitting agent, or authorize release of exclusively temporary references under existing fences. It cannot erase another owner pin or the last required copy by calling the nomination rejected. An externally held local-agent backlog is not this provider's admitted queue; if the provider stores it, its actual bytes, count and lifecycle must be admitted.

The rule supplies no unbounded hidden staging queue. If the next inspection cannot fit its finite slot and existing obligations, refuse/defer it before acquisition. No advertised future index entry, route row or discovery success may stand in for a later admission.

## A deliberately conservative computable admission rule

The procedure is sufficient, not necessary. It can reject a schedule that a cleverer planner could safely run. All scans and plan construction themselves use a separately bounded paid preparation share; the finite result is checked before its mutations start. Unsupported or incomplete state returns a local nondecision with the missing condition, not an optimistic estimate.

### Fixed ordering and bounded inputs

Use exact current state bounded by the supplied H count profile: at most 4,096 selected targets, 131,072 target/token incidences, 264,000 charged row records and 64 charged FeatureScopes, together with the actual smaller byte limits. Reserve incumbent custody/service, current group renewals, contact maintenance, already promised recovery, pending mutation reconciliation and mandatory cleanup first. A formerly discretionary repair share already committed to a real incident is not available for normalization. Enumerate due obligations by their actual local deadlines; a source cannot compare a foreign expiry timestamp directly with its own clock. The existing lease/headroom discipline and admitted remote bounds provide the needed observation-to-commit margins.

If a partial handoff exists, preserve its exact cursor and settle/reconcile it before starting a different split/carry. Otherwise, when no old-pool episode exists, freeze all currently pending inputs, at most 32, in their existing local admission order as one finite old pool. New arrivals cannot rename themselves into this pool or cause its cursor to restart. Apply O's canonical target partition and increasing-size/scopeID carries to that frozen pool and existing completed tiers. Disjoint semantic target identities, actual eligible anchors and unchanged foundation/extractor bindings are checked, not inferred from different root hashes.

At a settled logical cut the next frame first proposes the oldest two unprocessed original inputs, or the sole remaining one, with every resulting collision against actual occupied tiers. If that complete block cannot fit this planner's current finite envelope, propose the first still-unfinished O step only, in fixed windows of at most 4,096 row moves and its required logical/cleanup barriers. Scan its windows in order and admit the longest prefix whose conservative tests below pass; if even the first positive window or applicable zero-move commit cannot fit, defer. No new public cohort is admitted in this fallback partial-step frame. Bounded inspection/local work can proceed only with independent real residual supply. This fixed fallback is not a search for a globally optimal packing.

For a given frozen pool, construct its full logical split/carry plan before admitting its first mutation. Exactly count, or conservatively overbound, every actual owned-row move, group open/close, borrowed-owner resolution and final retirement. Four selected copies and at most 32 features per target give a conservative row-move bound only for the selected four-copy domain with no additional current/partial placement rows; they are not a general bound for every permitted H state. Every actual extra or partial row, deep marker and ancestor path, uncertain mutation and its possible surviving allocation requires its own counted stock, work, lease and cleanup term. Missing that exact inventory or a conservative bound returns `NEEDS_STATE`, rather than dropping the extra obligation. The positive case below explicitly uses depth-zero placement with no such extras. None of these bounds asserts that every feature is new or every group is singleton. Complete logical planning does not mean the whole plan's work grant exists: the actually admitted finite prefix and its safe continuation boundary are separately bound below. A later new completed run can change the remaining old pool's collisions; the next paid exact recut retains that pool's original identity, completed prefix, surviving obligations and spent work, but recomputes remaining debt against the new occupied tiers. It does not reuse a now-invalid future collision list or reset debt to zero.

### Finite resource test

Every elementary phase has a supplied vector of upper bounds for calls, CPU, I/O, network bytes and elapsed occupied service, together with maximum positive stock increments for physical groups/rows/scopes/bytes, joint residency and executor/batch occupancy. Examples are bounded inspection, one publication cohort, an O ownership-move window, a guarded logical step commit, actual tail settlement and a maintenance pass. The vectors include map/union construction, once-only histories, reply packing, staging, compaction and failure-path state, not only successful wire mutations. A missing upper bound fails this planner's test; it does not become zero.

Bind one exact measured physical admission cut b. For each subsequent phase prefix j and physical resource d, compute

`StockBound_d(b,j) = measured_stock_d(b) + sum(admitted_positive_d after b through j) − sum(credited_physical_release_d after b through j).`

Check the maximum of these bounds at every prescribed physical peak, including each phase's transient peak before any subsequent release. A release credit binds the unique actually charged allocation/storage generation and the actual resource quantity relinquished after b. It is available at most once per resource axis and only for quantity included in the measured base or an actual post-cut allocation covered by the admitted positive bound. A prior release already reflected in `measured_stock_d(b)` has no credit in this interval. Releasing one shared reference, logical CLOSE, expiry or intended deletion does not credit storage still allocated or pinned. Bytes and record counts are distinct resource axes; each receives only its own actual once-counted release. Uncertain release receives zero credit.

Before execution, count every planned release as zero. A plan may instead stop at an observed local release barrier, remeasure actual charged state and admit its successor from a new cut b'. This resets the stock base, positive-increment interval and release-credit interval together; the new ledger contains no pre-b' release credit. All work already spent, surviving references/uncertainty, plan/debt identity and remaining reservations still carry forward. For example, if a past 20-unit release has already reduced measured stock to 80, a new 30-unit allocation requires 110, not `80 + 30 − 20 = 90`; a 100-unit cap must reject it. This convention avoids both double-subtracted history and capacity inferred from a future timeout or remote GC assertion. Bytes deduplicate only when actual owned allocation is shared; reference records remain charged. Maximum simultaneous workspace and resident state are included, not inferred from disk size.

For every parent grant g and work resource d, require

`spent_d(g) + unspent_incumbent_reserve_d(g) + unspent_optional_reserve_d(g) + unspent_aftercare_reserve_d(g) ≤ actual_grant_d(g).`

These residual reservations are mutually disjoint and exclude already spent work. On actual expenditure, charge the parent grant's spent counter and consume the corresponding residual envelope; a completed prefix is not also retained as an unspent reservation. Keep uncertain calls charged under the inherited once-only spend rule. Count a work phase only once even if its elapsed block overlaps another role's waiting period. Shared physical hosts and provider ledgers need aggregate accounting, not one fresh budget per role. If a reserve is transferred between classes, only its actual uncommitted remainder transfers. Actual work spends grant tokens once; a lost reply or retry does not restore them. A stock recut does not reset this grant ledger.

Place renewal/deadline windows first. Pack optional phases in the prescribed order into the remaining explicitly supplied service blocks; this policy does not search arbitrary orderings. A phase may cross a funding boundary only when both real grants and its carried state, due obligations and elapsed constraints are already admitted. Verify its entire serial completion envelope and every required preemption against the available block. Include the largest nonpreemptible portion when checking maintenance deadlines. Tail barriers require actual bounded reference-fenced progress; idle hours do not prove it. Reject if any prefix exceeds a count, byte, resident, grant, executor, batch or deadline bound.

An immediate foreign mutation is still conditional on that holder's ordinary current authority and admission. The local plan cannot reserve foreign hardware by arithmetic. The positive trace assumes the named source/holder service actually holds; ordinary refusal follows the existing partial/failure path. Foreign garbage collection has no new acknowledgement operation here.

### Admission output and execution gate

The procedure returns one of a finite set of **local controller dispositions**, not new wire status codes:

- `ADMITTED_PREFIX`: exact frozen plan/cursor, counted state cut, paid finite steps, preemption and release barriers, and funded aftercare/next-decision cut.
- `INSPECT_ONLY`: only the bounded independent inspection/custody proposal is funded; no public selection has been promised.
- `DEFER_RESOURCE` or `NEEDS_STATE`: the named numerical envelope, deadline, lawful release, eligibility, exact partition or current supply condition fails or is unknown. Preserve all existing obligations and spent state.
- `SUPPLY_REQUIRED`: existing aftercare can be stated but no adequate successor service grant is present. This is an unresolved supply need, not a theorem that a new provider will appear.

At every barrier, compare actual work/stock/guards with the admitted remainder. When they differ, stop issuing new optional mutations, retain uncertain/partial state and the reserved incumbent/aftercare duties, and require a new explicit admission for any repair outside that remainder. A cost overrun can defeat the successful progress witness; it cannot authorize an overspend or fabricated completed cover. Failure beyond the assumed finite reserve/service envelope is reported as such. The policy cannot guarantee continuing service against arbitrary physical loss or unbounded denied resources.

Publication of a newly selected target has the strict existing order: independently checked complete evidence; actual ACTIVE replacement/added custody protection; current complete-closure PUBLIC_SERVE authority; guarded public index/postings commit; then ordinary row placement. A partial placement is reported partial. A new immutable custody scope is activated before the owner authorizes release of its predecessor; no in-place scope mutation or acquisition receipt substitutes for that release authority.

## Normalization debt, progress priority and endpoint obligations

Let a frozen pool's **migration debt** be the exact finite sum of its remaining successful owned-row moves under the O/H plan. Retain separately the remaining split/commit/retirement steps, unsatisfied release barriers and actually consumed/retry work. The debt is a cost upper envelope and cursor, not transferable currency or mathematical value. Replanning after eligibility or topology changes produces a new explicitly paid plan and preserves already spent work and surviving state; it never decrements debt by abandoning a pending scope without lawful disposition.

New public selection is subordinate to completion of the frame's committed old-pool progress prefix. For the concrete frame below, both specified old inputs must genuinely settle before the additional cohort is published. If normalization fails, its reserved new-publication resources may support separately authorized incumbent recovery, but the candidate does not bypass the barrier. Merely scheduling the same first step for tomorrow cannot unlock another day's arrivals. More generally, serialize completion of the selected normalization prefix, then guarded new selection, then the next exact plan cut; new borrowers/postings do not silently modify a frozen O owner/obligation plan. If the selected prefix leaves a physical handoff partial, no newcomers enter that plan, and a successor must settle/revalidate it first.

For a general backlog episode, retain a positive minimum completed prefix per admitted frame: at least one actual row-move window, or, when no row move remains for the next step, its actual logical commit and applicable release barrier. Bind the next decision to the current frame's funded suffix, before its due obligations lose service. Frames that complete no required prefix do not accept new public nominations under this policy. They may continue paid inspection or local investigation only within separately admitted stock; neither is misreported as normalization progress.

This is not by itself a queue stability theorem. A one-row window may be much slower than incoming work. Before accepting an offered continuing cadence, separately compare its complete newly introduced work/debt and upkeep envelope against supplied service over the proposed finite arrival horizon. Refuse that cadence if this conservative test fails. In particular, accepting four targets below does not renew P's twenty/day offer. A cumulative successful-debt cursor supplies conditional finite backlog liveness only when the owner actually funds enough subsequent windows, the finite frozen pool is not expanded, and the actual release/remote/native conditions continue to hold. Under new arrivals, churn or replanning, additionally require an eventually fixed finite eligible plan, or an explicit bound on cumulative added old-pool work that supplied successful progress actually overtakes. A finite bound for each separate recut is not such a cumulative bound: infinitely many individually bounded additions can prevent completion. The progress argument below states the corresponding precise condition. No fair global task scheduler is required.

## Conditional preservation and progress arguments

**Finite old-pool progress.** Expand a fixed finite eligible plan into its finite prescribed milestones: exact row-move settlements, exact logical step commits and exact required reference-fenced cleanup barriers.
