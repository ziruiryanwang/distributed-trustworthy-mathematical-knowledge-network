# Published-batch normalization into dyadic owner runs

Date: 2026-09-20. Author: `qualification_contract_review`. Status: proposed bounded owner-local contract, held for independent review; not selected or qualified. Concept only: no implementation, executable model, extraction, native replay or measurement. Frozen H/N and their audits remain unchanged.

## Exact missing choice and boundary

[H](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001H-resource-supply-and-discovery.md) permits a bounded initial batch but selects power-of-two completed owner runs and equal-size whole-run merges. [N](routing-recovery-contract.md) admits eight already-published twenty-target scopes as finite pending inputs. Repeated whole-run equal merges of those inputs have sizes 20·2^k, never powers of two. N's finite witness is valid; paying a later merge bill alone does not specify how these particular inputs enter H's binary tiers.

Select for investigation a **finite pending-input normalizer**, not another routing protocol or global scheduler. Given one already-published pending batch, it partitions its exact target set into dyadic child scopes, moves its actual publication ownership under existing F/G operations, and hands the children to ordinary H equal-size carries. It also covers a future batch before publication, where no old rows need moving. This note's positive case handles the already-published inputs, not only a prohibition on future twenty-target batches.

Mathematical objects, native evidence, public target postings and extractor meanings are unchanged. Only immutable publication-scope organization and its actual route owners change. No public research task, human-intention field, global roster, mandatory publication or new wire/native schema is introduced.

## Admission: exact logical partition, physical obligations separately

One explicit owner-authorized invocation binds the provider, FoundationID, ExtractorID, pending parent FeatureScope P, exact current target/descriptor/feature versions, publication-owner ledger, actual row/group observations, authority/disclosure/native guards, and finite stock/work/calendar allocations. The existing single writer fences that selected local partition cut. This operation shares H's **one active consolidation-handoff allowance**, not a separate split allowance. Concurrent contributions may enter independently admitted pending state but cannot mutate the frozen inputs or run another split/merge handoff against them.

Use G's exact semantic `targetRef` identity, not a proof-instance, replay or public-descriptor ID. Each target's actual chosen evidence and descriptors remain separately bound. Require:

- P has 1 ≤ n ≤ 4096 sorted, duplicate-free targetRefs; the actual qualified H profile and all smaller applicable limits bind.
- Those targetRefs are disjoint from the logical target sets of all other admitted runs/pending inputs in this collection. The source ledger has a receiver-owned exact partition inventory; missing/uncertain inventory yields `NEEDS_PARTITION_STATE`.
- Each target has complete current deterministic feature extraction under the same F/E, actual public postings and required protected native support. Changed evidence/descriptor/eligibility is a guarded update, not a second new target.
- Parent-owned keys, externally borrowed keys, complete target/token incidence, actual old holders/cells/group roots/versions and unresolved outcomes are known within the admitted traversal. An unresolved prior handoff is settled or kept fenced, not normalized around by guessing ownership.

An already-published overlap or duplicate yields `OVERLAPPING_INPUT` for this normalizer; it does not silently delete a target, row, proof or custody obligation. A separate explicitly admitted overlap/deduplication operation would be needed. This deliberately selected domain covers N's distinct-target example. An empty input has no dyadic run; close any genuinely obsolete operational state only through its existing funded cleanup, not by inventing a one-target child.

Treat an active parent and its provisional children as **one logical target partition** during transition. Physically, all parent/child scope records, target lists, token unions, snapshots, maps, pins, outcomes and reservations remain separately charged. Retirement removes a scope from the logical partition only at the specified local commit; it does not release its physical references.

Before physical work, construct a complete bounded plan from that frozen receiver-owned state. It includes target blocks, complete child token unions, old/new key owners and anchors, actual row moves and group reuse/open/close decisions, carry collisions, byte/count peaks, operation/work reserves, recurring renewals, cleanup and recovery. An initial plan may traverse at most the actual H collection's 4096 logical targets, 131072 target/token incidences and 264000 charged membership records, within its independently supplied CPU/I/O/byte bounds; unrelated larger state gives nondecision, not a free scan. Exact scope/hash construction and full disjointness/union checks are paid preparation. A sample planning workspace ceiling is 16 MiB; if the exact plan cannot fit, decline rather than emit an unchecked next-array or a silently truncated child set.

Unknown fit is not zero cost. No row mutation occurs before full-plan admission and version revalidation. The invocation and every remote call retain inherited once-only outcomes; a failure/reboot/timer never resets grants. After mutation begins, failure preserves a counted partial lineage; new work requires actual remaining authority or explicit readmission.

## Deterministic partition and token owner plan

Read P's targetRefs in their existing canonical order. Repeatedly take the largest power of two no greater than the remaining count, consuming that many consecutive targets. For n = 20 the blocks have sizes 16 and 4. They are nonempty, disjoint and exhaust P. The number of blocks is popcount(n), at most floor(log2 n)+1.

For each block construct the exact unchanged G record

`FeatureScope = [1,FoundationID,ExtractorID,orderedTargetRefs,emittedTokenUnion]`.

Its union includes every extracted token of every target in the block, whether owned here or borrowed. Reuse actual unchanged descriptor/feature bytes only under their exact current bindings; union construction, inventories and hashing still cost work. A dyadic parent can remain the same immutable scope and enter the ready queue without gratuitous renaming or physical movement.

For each key currently owned by P:

1. Prefer its existing eligible signed anchor and assign ownership to the unique child containing that anchor's exact target. The anchor must really emit this key under F/E and retain its current complete descriptor/replay/disclosure chain.
2. If that anchor cannot lawfully continue, choose the lexicographically least eligible `(child targetRef, public descriptor CID, replay CID)` supporting the key in P's fixed cut. The child's identity follows that target. This is only a deterministic choice among actually eligible records, not permission to repair a withdrawn/invalid root for free. Missing support gives `NEEDS_ELIGIBLE_ANCHOR` before mutation, or a counted degraded transition afterward.
3. Other children emitting the same key borrow coverage from the chosen child under H's ordinary provider-wide ledger. No second child/group gains ownership of that provider/key row by copying a receipt.

Keys owned by a scope outside P remain externally borrowed. The normalizer neither moves their rows nor assigns them to a new child. Every child retains explicit owner obligations for all its emitted tokens. Borrowing does not make that child independently FULL_FEATURE; H's aggregate coverage and G's own scope status retain their distinct meanings.

Conversely, a P-owned key can have borrowers outside P. Preserve those obligations through H's provider-wide stable `(provider,F,E,key)` owner-ledger entry, with exact current owner/transition version, rather than replacing an unbounded array of borrower scopes. Borrowers resolve that entry under current guards; cached coverage binds its actual version and cannot stay current after a changed owner/transition. On entry to an in-flight owner transition, before dispatching its first ownership-changing mutation, the existing guarded local authority boundary marks the owner entry transitional and advances its owner/transition version. Every settled-coverage assertion or cache derived from the earlier version thereby loses current status, including external borrowers' cached aggregates, before any mixed placement and before final logical partition commit. Historical observations remain historical; surviving individual copies are not a settled aggregate claim. Recomputing any cached aggregate or traversing reverse dependents is separately metered, bounded work. If the existing local state instead embeds unversioned copied owner references, require a paid complete compatible reference-update plan or decline this normalizer; do not assume an implicit indirection exists. New physical placements, the owner entry and its invalidating version/transition status are published at the existing guarded local authority boundary, never as an old owner plus a misleading settled new-cover bit.

[ProviderAd2](../../../notes/analyses/CP-001G-profile-and-consumer-bindings.md) is `[2,providerNodeID,K,revision,publicContact,anchorPublicDescriptorCID,anchorReplayID]`: **there is no scopeID in the ad**. ScopeID belongs to immutable group state. If the same eligible anchor and every other bound ad field remains unchanged, this scope migration can preserve the exact signed ad bytes under F's revision rules. It still needs actual new-group authority and old DROP/new PUT. A changed anchor/contact or other signed field requires the existing higher revision and revalidation; no equal-revision/different-bytes substitution is allowed.

## Physical migration and truthful interruption

Use G operation 20 for a new FULL_FEATURE group, followed by F operations 17–19 with exact mode/F/E and current member roots/versions. No generic CAS read or native checking shortcut follows. Order planned keys, cells and holders canonically. Migrate one selected row at a time, subject to existing group serialization, and perform only the currently authorized next action:

1. Reserve the actual destination group/row/map and old/new overlap, reply, history, retention and settlement work. Reuse an active exact child group only under its real state. OPEN alone supplies neither future row capacity nor a fresh lease on an existing group.
2. At the same holder/cell/provider key, complete the exact old-group DROP before the new-group PUT. No cross-group atomicity or make-before-break is claimed. Keep other actual copies eligible where possible; an intervening local gap is disclosed, not erased by intended ownership.
3. Derive each next expected group root/version from reconciled receiver-owned observations. A lost reply is not failure of the mutation: consult the exact retained outcome/expected transition. While unresolved, do not assume the row is absent, submit a conflicting owner, repeat a spent operation or count its destination as placed.
4. Apply the eligible ad to the child group only after the old ownership fence is resolved. Record the actual per-row old/new scope owner, ad and generation. A token whose required copies are still mixed is TRANSFERRING/PARTIAL, not settled single-owner coverage.
5. CLOSE a parent group only after every row it owns has lawfully moved/withdrawn and its exact empty state is known. Remaining parent keys keep their old scope/anchor eligibility and supplied renewals. Closing an empty group still needs its own paid call; delayed cleanup remains generation-fenced and charged.

Same-holder migration can fail after DROP. The missing copy remains missing; automatic rollback or an invented parent row is forbidden. Other current replicas may still supply a path, but no uninterrupted-availability guarantee follows. An explicit later paid action may finish/replan under current guards. Revocation or incident stops the affected publication path even when old bytes remain; it does not delete independently owned proof custody.

Known partial shallow/deep placements, empty groups, snapshot tails and uncertain allocations are included in the physical plan. Deep paths also retain separately funded actual ancestor markers; group renewal never renews them. The small numerical case below is depth zero, not an implicit deep-path bound.

Current source eligibility is checked before every admission/renewal and applicable commit; dirty summaries require paid revalidation. During the lineage, supply continuing old/new group leases, authenticated provider authority and all other owners' obligations. Snapshot observations remain historical. A successful group response does not establish future placement or native usability.

After every required ownership move and borrowed-owner obligation has its selected settled state, one guarded local transaction replaces P's **logical** partition entry by its exact children and updates the per-token owner/borrower ledger. The old immutable P and its operational history remain physically retained through actual retirement. Children colliding with occupied tiers are `READY_FOR_CARRY`, not a second completed run at that size. Physical tail retirement and scope-count release use actual existing cleanup; an uncertain tail is counted until resolved.

This lineage uses H's one handoff allowance throughout; at most one split or two-parent carry step has physical row migration in flight. Earlier step tails remain charged while later steps proceed only if the shared stock/authority plan permits. No fresh handoff slot is created per child. If capacity requires waiting for tails, wait or decline further work without relabeling the incomplete operation complete.

Publication-scope ownership changes invalidate the **affected** N cut/bound. Admit the resulting obligations and calendar anew before asserting their N recovery bound. Unaffected main-scope obligations retain their own guards. This is different from N's allowed movement of physical holders within an unchanged publication owner. No whole-network invalidation or fresh funding follows from a scope hash.

## Equal-size carries and the finite progress argument

Once a split step is logically settled, process its dyadic ready runs by increasing size, then exact scopeID. Under the same frozen admitted collection/lineage, either install a ready run in an empty completed tier, or combine it with that tier's unique incumbent into the exact disjoint union. The latter is H's existing equal-size carry: construct one immutable union scope, transfer precisely the parents' actually owned keys, preserve externally borrowed keys, keep eligible anchors (now inside the union), settle observed ownership, then retire parents logically. A physical/authority failure stops this attempt with counted ready/partial state; it cannot leave two completed incumbents at one size.

Every carry and its largest possible union must fit the selected 4096-target profile, actual token/row/group limits and the admitted complete work calendar. Further collisions repeat only within the original finite plan and remaining grants. New arrivals not in that plan remain pending. `NEEDS_LARGER_SLOT` or `DEFER_RESOURCE` does not authorize a new server, larger grant or local data eviction; H's explicitly willing funded slot expansion is a separate admission.

For a finite disjoint pool of total M targets, assume all selected normalization/carry steps actually settle and no new input enters that pool. Every child size is a power of two. Every carry preserves mass and replaces two size-2^k runs by one size-2^(k+1) run. Let R count all dyadic completed and ready runs and Q count ready runs. A carry strictly decreases R; installing in an empty tier preserves R and decreases Q. Thus lexicographic `(R,Q)` decreases for each successful carry/install step. An unresolved operation is not a successful decrease.

The final unique-size completed runs sum to M, hence their occupied sizes are exactly M's binary expansion: final run count is popcount(M), no more than floor(log2 M)+1. Given R0 dyadic runs after normalization, there are exactly R0 − popcount(M) successful carries. These are finite termination/count facts conditional on actual paid steps, not wall-clock, availability or sustained-rate theorems. Physically retained parents and uncertain records are excluded from R but included in resource stock.

Splitting can increase run count from one to popcount(n); charge that operation separately. For a key actually owned by the normalized parent, normalization requires at most one successful ownership move per retained placement row to its selected child, followed by ordinary carries. Each subsequent carry doubles that owner's target-count scale, giving at most floor(log2(M/m)) further successful owner moves from a child of size m within that fixed insertion-only epoch. Failed/retried calls, additional copies, anchor churn, scope splitting and repair are additional actual work. This preserves H's conditional logarithmic carry argument without retroactively calling normalization free or reapplying it indefinitely to the same input.

## Failure cases and review boundary

- Same semantic target appears in another logical input: refuse this partition operation; keep all published/custody obligations intact.
- Token appears in both children but its old eligible anchor lies in the first: first child owns it, second borrows; no duplicate group owner or fabricated second ad.
- The same parent-owned token is also used by an unrelated scope X: X keeps its stable owner-ledger reference. During mixed movement its dependent coverage is transitional, and after settlement it resolves the selected child. An old X coverage cache bound to the earlier owner version is not current. Updating that shared entry does not move X's unrelated keys or renew its groups for free.
- Chosen child cannot support its planned anchor, disclosure changes or a native incident arises: stop/degrade and require a new actual eligible plan, not public continuity by bookkeeping.
- DROP commits and its reply is lost: preserve the exact uncertainty; neither repeat the spent mutation nor claim new ownership before reconciliation.
- A carry is ready but parent tails fill stock, repair consumes the discretionary reserve, or lease headroom fails: preserve counted ready/partial state and defer. A ready run is not a second completed tier or free future job.
- Repeating normalization on newly named copies of one input cannot create new target mass or authority. Local partition/invocation fences and actual retained obligations prevent that shortcut.
- An endless arrival stream faster than paid normalization/carry/expansion can remain backlogged or be declined. The potential proof covers a finite admitted pool, not automatic sustainable throughput or useful exponential growth.

The proposed addition closes only the non-dyadic-input continuation choice. It does not qualify BOOT1, native export/application, real resource costs, global discovery or causal mathematical benefit. Independent composition and resource/lifecycle reviews must bind an exact held source before selection.
